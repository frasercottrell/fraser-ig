# Batch 16. Day 5 (3/day) - proven reach formula HELD, CTA is the variable (crack conversion)

## Why this batch exists
After Day 3 (batch 15) the reach/retention machine is solved: record skip (21-27%), record watch (9-10s),
2,000+ reach per reel. But across ~25 reels: **Follows 0, Comments 0.** Reach without conversion.
So Day 5 stops iterating the hook and starts iterating the CTA.

## What's HELD constant (the proven reach formula - do not change)
- **Arresting / tight first frame.** Busy, scrappy, "what's that?" workbench-style footage stopped hardest (Day 3). Congruence with the message is a bonus. Retire nothing that's working.
- **Sharp, relatable, universal angle** in a proven family (F1 contrarian / F2 competitive-FOMO / F3 pattern-authority).
- **5-point save-worthy caption**, Fraser's voice, em-dash-free. These hold watch to 9-10s.
- **Trending audio (↑).**

## What's being TESTED (the variable)
Three different CTAs, one per reel, to attack the two conversion gaps (comments + follows):
- **Reel 5.1 = CTA-A: low-friction comment prompt.** A one-tap "comment the number" question instead of a keyword. Hypothesis: the HOOKS keyword is too much friction cold; a number is the lowest-effort comment possible, and comments snowball reach.
- **Reel 5.2 = CTA-B: explicit follow-reason.** Name the series + cadence + what they get by following. Hypothesis: cold viewers don't follow because we never gave them a concrete reason, only a lead magnet.
- **Reel 5.3 = CTA-C: sharpened keyword + DM mechanic.** Keep the HOOKS lead magnet but make the value vivid and the mechanic obvious (one word, I DM it). Hypothesis: the offer works, the wording didn't.

Log as reels 026-028. Track COMMENTS and FOLLOWS as the primary metric this time (reach is expected to hold).

## Reel 5.1 - F3 Pattern/authority - CTA-A (low-friction comment prompt)
**On-screen hook:** "I've made 10,000 ads. These 5 hooks still beat everything."
**B-roll:** tight/busy workbench or desk, hands on gear / flipping a notebook of hooks. Arresting first frame, fill it.

**Caption:**

Save this before you write your next ad 👇

I've written more ad hooks than I can count. Trends come and go, but these 5 formats have never stopped working. Steal them, then tell me which one you're leaning on:

1. The callout. "[Exact person], you're making this mistake." Names who it's for and hits a nerve in the first second. "DTC founders burning £50k a month, this is for you." The right person feels caught. Everyone else scrolls, and that's fine.

2. The number. "I analysed 500 ads and found one pattern." A specific figure plus a payoff you don't give away yet. Specificity reads as proof, and the open loop keeps them watching for the answer.

3. The contrarian. "Everyone says X. It's wrong." A pattern interrupt that argues with what the viewer already believes, so now they have to know why. Tension is attention.

4. The confession. "The biggest mistake I made cost me thousands." Vulnerability lowers the guard and makes a stranger lean in. People trust someone admitting a loss more than someone claiming a win.

5. The reveal. "The one thing every winning ad does." You name a pattern and hide the answer. The only way to close the loop is to keep watching. Curiosity does the work.

Every one of these opens on the viewer, never the product. That's the thread.

## Reel 5.2 - F2 Competitive/FOMO - CTA-B (explicit follow-reason)
**On-screen hook:** "Your competitor isn't more creative than you. They just test 10x faster."
**B-roll:** tight/busy workbench, hands assembling gear / two ads side by side on a screen. Arresting first frame.

**Caption:**

Save this before you blame your creative team 👇

The brand beating you isn't full of geniuses. They've just built a faster loop, and speed compounds. Here's what they do that you don't:

1. They separate testing from producing. Cheap, rough tests first to find the angle. Budget goes to production only after something proves it works. You spend big up front and hope. They spend big only on winners.

2. They decide fast. A weak ad is cut in days, not defended for a month. No meetings about whether to kill it, the numbers decide. Speed of decision is their real edge, not talent.

3. They keep a backlog. There's always a next ad ready to go. When one fades, the replacement is already live, so there's never a gap. You go quiet after a winner dies. They never stop.

4. They learn in public. Every shipped ad teaches them something about the customer. 40 ads a month is 40 lessons. You're still perfecting ad number three and learning nothing until it's live.

5. They treat it as a system, not a spark. They're not waiting for a creative genius moment. They've built a machine that produces, tests, and iterates on repeat. Systems beat inspiration every time.

You don't need more creativity. You need a faster loop. Start by lowering the bar for what you ship.

## Reel 5.3 - F1 Contrarian - CTA-C (sharpened keyword + DM mechanic)
**On-screen hook:** "My best-performing ad this year took 20 minutes to make."
**B-roll:** tight/busy workbench, hands grabbing a phone / rough phone-shot setup. Arresting first frame.

**Caption:**

Save this before you spend another week on one ad 👇

My highest-performing ad this year was filmed on a phone in 20 minutes. It beat ads we spent thousands on. Here's why the rough one won, and what it taught me:

1. It looked like content, not an ad. No polish, no logo, no intro. The brain didn't flag it as an advert, so people watched instead of scrolling. Polish is often what gives you away.

2. It opened on a real problem. Straight into a frustration the viewer felt, in plain words, in the first line. No setup. The expensive ads spent 3 seconds on branding and lost everyone before the point.

3. It said one thing. One problem, one message, hammered. The big-budget ads tried to show off everything and landed nothing. Constraint made it clear.

4. It was made fast enough to test. Because it took 20 minutes, we could make ten more like it. The polished ad was a single expensive bet. The rough one was a cheap experiment we could run again and again.

5. It proved the point that matters. Money doesn't make ads work. The right message, on the right problem, shown to the right person does. Budget just scales what already works, or wastes faster what doesn't.

Stop over-producing. Make it rough, make it clear, make ten.
