## Caption (long, save-worthy)

Save this before you kill your next "cringe" ad 👇

When I worked in filmmaking we had a saying: kill your darlings. Sounds brutal. What it means is the shot you love most, the one you spent all day lighting, the one you're proud of, has to go if it doesn't serve the story. Your attachment to it is not a reason to keep it.

Ad creative works exactly the same way. Honestly, anything you make does. The problem is your opinion of your own work is clouded by things your customer never sees:

1. Effort. You rate the ad you sweated over higher than the one you filmed in ten minutes. The customer has no idea how long anything took, and doesn't care.

2. Taste. "Beautiful" and "cringe" are your standards, not theirs. The ad that makes you wince is often the one that's converting.

3. Knowledge. You know the founder story, the sourcing, why the logo is that colour. Your customer knows none of it, so the thing you think is obvious lands completely differently.

4. Ego. Killing an ad you're proud of feels like admitting you were wrong. So people keep the darling running and quietly let it drag the account.

Here's the fix. Stop asking "do I like this ad" and start asking "what did the number do". Let the market vote, not your feelings. The ad you'd never have picked is usually the one worth scaling, and the darling you're protecting is usually the one to cut.

Your taste isn't the KPI. Your customer's behaviour is.
