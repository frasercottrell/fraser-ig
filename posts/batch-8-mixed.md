## 1. Pillar 1: Why Ads Work

**On-screen hook:** "Your ad has no idea who it's talking to. That's why no one stops."

**Caption:**

Save this before your next ad. How to write to one person instead of everyone 👇

An ad that talks to everyone stops no one. "Great for anyone who wants better skin" slides straight past. The ads that convert sound like they were made for one specific person, because they were.

1. Name them out loud. Not "our customers". The exact person. The 34-year-old who's tried three creams and given up.
2. Use their words, not yours. The way they'd describe it to a mate, not the way your brand deck describes it.
3. Talk about their day, not your product. Where the problem shows up. The moment it bites.

Write to one person. Everyone else who fits will feel it too.

## 2. Pillar 2: Inside the Ad World (AI, operator-framed)

**On-screen hook:** "Everyone's using AI to write their ads. It's the one thing it's worst at."

**Caption:**

Save this. Where AI actually helps in ad creative, and the one place it quietly ruins it 👇

AI is brilliant at the grunt work and terrible at the part that decides whether the ad works. Get it backwards and you'll ship a hundred polished ads that all sound the same.

Where it earns its keep:
1. Research. Mining reviews, pulling angles, spotting patterns in your data, fast.
2. Variations. Ten cuts, ten formats, ten thumbnails from one idea.
3. The repetitive stuff. Resizing, rough edits, localising.

Where it kills you:
4. The hook and the script. AI writes the average of every ad ever made, and average doesn't stop a thumb. The words a human reads have to come from a human, or a real customer.

Use AI for the reps. Keep the thinking.

## 3. Pillar 4: Founder Perspective  ·  soft CTA

**On-screen hook:** "You're the bottleneck on your own ads. And you don't even know it."

**Caption:**

Save this before your next round of creative feedback. How to give notes that make ads better, not worse 👇

Most founders kill good ads with bad feedback. "I don't like it" isn't feedback. "Make it more premium" isn't either. You're editing to your taste, and your taste isn't the customer.

Better notes:
1. React to the hook, not the whole thing. If the first 3 seconds don't stop you, nothing else matters yet.
2. Ask "is this clear?" not "do I like it?" Clarity is measurable. Taste isn't.
3. Point at the customer, not yourself. "Would they get this in one watch?" beats "I'd have done it differently."

Your job isn't to design the ad. It's to protect the clarity.

## 4. Pillar 1: Why Ads Work  ·  soft CTA

**On-screen hook:** "You're scaling the wrong ad. The numbers are lying to you."

**Caption:**

Save this before you pour budget into your next "winner" 👇

A high ROAS on day 2 isn't a winner. It's a small sample and a lucky day. Scale it too early and it falls apart, and you blame the ad instead of the read.

What to actually check before you scale:
1. Spend behind it. 20 conversions minimum before you trust the number. Less than that is noise.
2. Is it still working today, not just on the launch spike? Winners hold. Flukes fade.
3. Can you say why it worked? If you can't, you can't rebuild it when it dies. And it will die.

Scale what you understand, not what got lucky.

## 5. Pillar 1: Why Ads Work

**On-screen hook:** "Your hooks are too clever to work."

**Caption:**

Save this before you write your next hook. Why clever loses to clear, every time 👇

Clever hooks make the writer feel smart. Clear hooks make the customer feel seen. Only one of those stops the scroll.

The wordplay, the pun, the double meaning. The viewer has to decode it, and decoding is effort, and effort is a scroll. They're not going to work for it. You've got one second.

1. Say the thing straight. "Your ads are dying in the first 3 seconds" beats any clever spin on it.
2. Lead with the pain, not the punchline.
3. If it needs a second read to land, it's already lost.

Boring and clear beats clever and confusing. Save the wit for once they're watching.
