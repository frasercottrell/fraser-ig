## Caption

Save this before your next shoot 👇

We scaled a dry skin brand 70% with close-up photos of people's feet. Not lifestyle shots. Not the product on a clean background. Feet, badly lit, filmed on a phone. The client hated it and it outperformed everything we'd made for them. Here's what that taught me about problem-led creative:

1. Show the problem at its worst, not the product at its best. Most brands photograph the solution and hope you infer the problem. Flip it. The person scrolling doesn't recognise your product, they recognise their own cracked heels. Recognition is what stops the scroll, and you can't get recognition from a hero shot.

2. Don't flatter the before. Every before-and-after I've seen fail had a before that was already fine. If the before looks acceptable, there's nothing to fix and no reason to buy. The uncomfortable version is the one that works, because it's the honest one.

3. Match the format to how people actually see the problem. Nobody encounters their own dry skin under studio lighting. They see it in a bathroom at 7am on a phone screen. Shoot it the way they see it and the ad reads as real rather than staged.

4. The ad your client hates is worth testing anyway. Founders and marketing teams judge creative on whether it represents the brand well. Customers judge it on whether it's about them. Those are different tests, and only one of them is the one that pays. Run it as a test rather than arguing about it.

5. This only works when the buyer is problem-aware. Someone who already knows they have the problem responds to seeing it named. Someone who doesn't will just find it unpleasant. Check where your buyer actually sits before you go ugly, because the same ad that scales one account will tank another.

The uncomfortable truth is that the ads we're proudest of are made for the client, and the ads that scale are made for the customer. They're rarely the same ad.
