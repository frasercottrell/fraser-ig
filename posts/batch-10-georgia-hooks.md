## 3. "Stop copying viral ads." (her Contrarian) · HOOKS

**On-screen hook:** "Stop copying viral ads."
**B-roll (arresting):** tight, thumb fast-scrolling a phone full of ads. Movement, close.
**Audio:** trending (↑).

**Caption:**

Save this before you rip off another viral ad 👇

Copying a viral ad almost never works, because you copy the wrong part. You take the format, the trend, the edit, and miss the thing that actually made it land.

What to steal instead:

1. The insight, not the execution. Why did it resonate? What truth did it name? Take that.
2. The structure, not the script. The order it revealed things. The way it opened the loop.
3. The emotion, not the aesthetic. What did it make people feel? Recreate that with your product.

A viral ad is a case study, not a template. Work out why it worked, then build your own.

## 5. "Your product isn't the problem. Your messaging is." (her Founders) · HOOKS

**On-screen hook:** "Your product isn't the problem. Your messaging is."
**B-roll (arresting):** an unusual, busy set moment (paddling-pool energy). Clear centred subject.
**Audio:** trending (↑).

**Caption:**

Save this before you blame the product 👇

When sales stall, founders reach for the product. New feature, new packaging, new variant. Usually the product was fine. Nobody understood why it mattered, because the messaging never told them.

Three signs it's your messaging, not your product:

1. People who try it love it, but you can't get them to try it. That's a messaging gap, not a product gap.
2. You explain it in person and they get it, but the ad doesn't say the same thing.
3. You lead with what it is, not what it does for them. Features, not the outcome they actually want.

Fix the words before you touch the product. It's cheaper, and it's usually the real problem.

## 6. "You're driving traffic to an offer that isn't exciting anyone." (her Ecommerce) · HOOKS

**On-screen hook:** "You're driving traffic to an offer that isn't exciting anyone."
**B-roll (arresting):** tight, a product being set up / handled on set. Clear subject, movement.
**Audio:** trending (↑).

**Caption:**

Save this before you spend another penny on traffic 👇

Great ads pointing at a boring offer is money on fire. The ad does its job, the click happens, and then the offer kills the sale. You blame the ad. It wasn't the ad.

What makes an offer worth stopping for:

1. A reason to act now. Not a permanent 10% off. A real reason today beats tomorrow.
2. More than the product. A bonus, a guarantee, a bundle. Stack the value so the price feels small.
3. One clear thing. If I have to work out what I'm getting, you've lost me.

Fix the offer and the same ads suddenly convert. Traffic was never the problem.
