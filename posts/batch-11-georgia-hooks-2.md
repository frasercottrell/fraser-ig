## 1. "Here's why this Nike campaign worked." (her Ad Breakdown, brand) · HOOKS

**On-screen hook:** "Here's why this Nike campaign worked."
**B-roll:** watching the Nike ad on a big screen / phone held up. Tight on the screen.

**Caption:**

Save this 👇

Nike almost never talks about the shoe. They talk about who you become when you stop making excuses.

The campaign that worked did three quiet things:

1. It picked an enemy. Not a competitor. A feeling. Doubt, the voice that says "tomorrow". You against that.
2. It made you the hero, not the brand. Nike's just the thing in your hand while you do the hard thing.
3. It earned the logo. By the time the swoosh lands, you already feel something. The brand is the full stop, not the sentence.

You're not Nike. But you can pick an enemy, make the customer the hero, and earn your logo.

## 2. "I'd give this ad a 4/10." (her Ad Breakdown, rating format) · soft CTA

**On-screen hook:** "I'd give this ad a 4/10."
**B-roll:** reacting to an ad on a screen, tight. Or scrubbing it on a monitor.

**Caption:**

Save this before your next ad goes live 👇

Decent production. Nice product. Still a 4. Here's what dragged it down, and what most brands get wrong the exact same way:

1. It opened on the brand, not the problem. Three seconds gone before it gave me a reason to care.
2. It listed features. Nobody buys a list. They buy the one thing that fixes their problem.
3. No reason to keep watching. It told me everything up front, so I left.

Same footage, open on the problem, pick one message, hold something back. That's an 8.

## 3. "Most brands are wasting money because of this." (her Educational) · HOOKS

**On-screen hook:** "Most brands are wasting money because of this."
**B-roll:** an unusual set moment or reviewing spend on a bright screen.

**Caption:**

Save this before your next campaign 👇

It's not the budget. It's not the targeting. It's that they run one ad and wait.

One ad means one hook, one angle, one shot at stopping a stranger. When it fades, and it always fades, they've got nothing ready and growth stalls.

The fix isn't more money. It's more angles. Three ads testing three different reasons to care will beat one ad with a bigger budget behind it, almost every time.

Stop betting everything on one creative. Spread the bets.

## 5. "If you can only sell with a discount, you don't have a brand yet." (her Ecommerce) · HOOKS

**On-screen hook:** "If you can only sell with a discount, you don't have a brand yet."
**B-roll:** arresting, product being handled on set.

**Caption:**

Save this before you run another sale 👇

Discounts feel like they work because they move product today. But if the only reason people buy is the code, you've taught them to never pay full price. That's not a brand. That's rented demand.

What sells without a discount:

1. A reason it's worth it at full price. What makes it better, said plainly.
2. Proof. Real people, real results, not your own claims.
3. A story people want to be part of, not just a product they want cheap.

Discounts should be a lever you choose, not the only one that works.

## 6. "Great ads aren't creative for the sake of being creative." (her Contrarian) · HOOKS

**On-screen hook:** "Great ads aren't creative for the sake of being creative."
**B-roll:** arresting set/edit moment, tight.

**Caption:**

Save this before your next "big idea" 👇

Somewhere along the way creative got confused with clever. Awards, twists, jokes nobody asked for. Meanwhile the ad that actually sells is often almost boring.

Creativity in an ad has one job: make a real message impossible to ignore. Not to be admired. To be understood in a second. And felt.

1. If the clever bit distracts from the message, cut it.
2. If a stranger can't tell what you're selling in 3 seconds, it's art, not an ad.
3. The best "creative" idea is usually just the clearest one.

Serve the message. That's the whole job.
