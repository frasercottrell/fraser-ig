# Talking-Head Hooks + Topics (spoken)

For talking-head videos (the convert assets). The hook = what Fraser SAYS in the first 3 seconds.
Written to be spoken, not read: start mid-thought, contractions, say it like you'd say it to a mate.
Georgia's rule for talking heads: all three hooks — verbal (say it) + text (same line on screen) + visual (you, doing something).
10–30 seconds. Grab a hook, riff the beats, land the close. Don't read it word-for-word — make it yours.

---

## 1. "I've made over 10,000 ads, and the best ones were almost always the ugliest."
**Topic:** ugly beats polished.
- The polished, cinematic ad screams "advertisement", and people scroll ads.
- The one that looks like a real person filmed it on their phone gets trusted before you've said a word.
- And you can make 20 of them for the cost of one "premium" one, so you find the winner faster.
**Close:** "Stop polishing. Start testing." → Follow / comment HOOKS.

## 2. "Most brands don't have an ad problem. They've got a first-line problem."
**Topic:** the hook is 80% of the result.
- The first three seconds decide everything. If they don't stop, nothing else you made even gets seen.
- Most people spread their effort evenly across the whole ad. Put it all into the opening.
- And open on the customer's problem, not your product. Nobody cares about your product yet.
**Close:** "Fix the first line before you touch anything else."

## 3. "If your agency can't tell you why your best ad worked, they didn't make it work. They got lucky."
**Topic:** accountability / the why-gap.
- A winner you can't explain is a coin flip. You can't repeat it.
- A real partner points at the ad and tells you the mechanism, then builds the next one on it.
- If every good result is a surprise and every bad one is "the algorithm", you're paying for guessing.
**Close:** "You should know why. Every week."

## 4. "Everyone's using AI to write their ads. It's the one thing AI is worst at."
**Topic:** where AI helps vs hurts (operator-framed).
- AI's brilliant at the grunt work — research, variations, edits.
- It's terrible at the hook and the script, because it writes the average of every ad ever made, and average doesn't stop a thumb.
- Use it for the reps. Keep the thinking.
**Close:** "The words a human reads have to come from a human."

## 5. "Stop copying viral ads. You're copying the part that doesn't transfer."
**Topic:** what to actually steal.
- People copy the format, the trend, the edit — and miss the thing that made it land.
- Steal the insight, not the execution. The structure, not the script. The emotion, not the look.
- A viral ad is a case study, not a template.
**Close:** "Work out why it worked, then build your own."

## 6. "Your best ad didn't stop working. Your audience just got sick of it — and you didn't see it coming."
**Topic:** creative fatigue.
- Every winner fatigues. Not because it got worse, because they've seen it too many times.
- The signs are there early: frequency climbing, CTR sliding, CPA creeping up.
- The fix isn't a better ad. It's the next one ready before this one drops.
**Close:** "You're not chasing one winner. You're building a pipeline."

## 7. "Here's the uncomfortable truth: you're the least qualified person to judge your own ads."
**Topic:** founders judging their own creative.
- You know too much. The founder story, the sourcing, why the logo's that colour. Your customer knows none of it.
- So when you kill the "cringe" ad that's actually converting, you're not protecting the brand. You're taxing it with your taste.
- Judge the ad by the number, not by whether it flatters how you see the brand.
**Close:** "Your taste is not the KPI."

## 8. "I can look at an ad account for thirty seconds and tell you if it's going to scale."
**Topic:** the fast audit.
- First thing I check isn't the targeting. It's the hook rate. If people don't stop, nothing downstream matters.
- Then variety — ten versions of the same ad, or ten different angles? Same idea on repeat isn't testing.
- Then the winning ad's age. If one ad's carried it for months, it's fatiguing and there's no replacement coming.
**Close:** "It's almost never the account. It's the creative."

## 9. "The best copywriter on your account isn't a copywriter. It's your customer."
**Topic:** voice-of-customer mining.
- The best-converting line I've ever used, I didn't write. A customer did, in a 3-star review.
- They describe the problem better than any copywriter, because they actually lived it.
- Mine your reviews, your DMs, your support tickets. Use their words, word for word.
**Close:** "You're not a copywriter. You're a translator."

## 10. "You don't need a bigger budget. You need more angles."
**Topic:** creative volume beats spend.
- One ad is one hook, one angle, one shot at stopping a stranger. When it fades, you stall.
- Three ads testing three different reasons to care will beat one ad with a bigger budget behind it.
- Spend amplifies what's working. If nothing's working, it just amplifies the leak.
**Close:** "Stop betting everything on one creative."

---

## Notes for filming
- **Say the hook flat and fast, then pause.** The stop happens on the claim, not on energy.
- **Mirror the spoken hook as on-screen text** (same words) — catches mute scrollers. Keep it em-dash-free.
- **One idea per video.** Don't cram. 10–30 seconds.
- **These are convert assets** (warmer audience) — lean into the opinion and the authority. More "you" than the trial reels.
- Cross-post the good ones to YouTube Shorts + TikTok.
