## Caption (long, save-worthy)

Save this before you waste another hour on manual research 👇

Most brands think they have an ad problem. Half the time they have a research problem, and they're doing all of it by hand.

The brands pumping out winners aren't more creative than you. They're faster at finding what to say. Here are the three research jobs you should never do manually again:

1. Deep customer research. Scrolling reviews one by one, taking notes in a doc, hoping you spot a pattern. Tools like Claude Code, ChatGPT and Codex can read hundreds of reviews, comments and support tickets at once and hand you the exact pains and phrases your customer uses. What took a day takes ten minutes.

2. Finding what's actually working. Most people scroll the ad library for hours guessing which ads are winning. You can't tell a winner from a flop by looking at it. A tool like Trendtrack shows you which ads are actually getting spend behind them, so you study the ones the brand is paying to keep live, not the ones that flopped last week.

3. Mining real customer language. Reddit is the best free focus group on earth, and people are still reading threads one at a time. Reddit Answers is an AI-powered search that pulls the relevant results for any topic in seconds, so you get the raw voice of your customer without the scroll.

Here's the part nobody says though. AI is brilliant at all three of these because they're research and reps. It is terrible at the one thing everyone tries to use it for: writing the actual hook and script. It writes the average of every ad ever made, and average doesn't stop a thumb.

So hand it the research. Keep the thinking.
