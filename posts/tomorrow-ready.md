- The goal loop: dynamic B-roll stops the stranger → saveable/AI topic earns the save/share → IG expands reach → new follows.

## 1 — AI (best engagement topic) · HOOKS

**On-screen hook:** "Everyone's using AI to write their ads. It's the one thing it's worst at."
**B-roll:** bright — you at the laptop mid-work, hands moving, screen visible. Movement, not a static sit.
**Audio:** trending (↑).

**Caption:**

Save this. Where AI actually helps in ad creative, and the one place it quietly ruins it 👇

AI is brilliant at the grunt work and terrible at the part that decides whether the ad works. Get it backwards and you'll ship a hundred polished ads that all sound the same.

Where it earns its keep:
1. Research. Mining reviews, pulling angles, spotting patterns in your data. Fast.
2. Variations. Ten cuts, ten formats, ten thumbnails from one idea.
3. The repetitive stuff. Resizing, localising, rough edits.

Where it kills you:
4. The hook and the script. AI writes the average of every ad ever made, and average doesn't stop a thumb. The words a human reads have to come from a human. Or a real customer.

Use AI for the reps. Keep the thinking.

## 2 — Hooks framework (HOOKS-perfect fit) · HOOKS

**On-screen hook:** "You're writing your hooks in the wrong order."
**B-roll:** the crew filming on set (the winner — camera on gimbal, someone directing, movement). Visually busy.
**Audio:** trending (↑).

**Caption:**

Save this before your next ad. The 3 jobs every hook has to do in the first 3 seconds, in order 👇

Most hooks open by talking about the product. That's exactly why they get skipped. The winners all do these three, in this order, before the third second:

1. Name the exact person. Not "everyone". "DTC founders spending £50k a month on Meta" beats "business owners". The right person has to feel you're talking to them.
2. Hit a pain they already feel. Not a feature. The tension that was in their head before your ad showed up. You're not creating it, you're naming it back to them.
3. Open a loop. Give them a reason the next three seconds are worth it. A claim or question they now need answered.

Person, pain, loop. Miss the order and the scroll wins.

## 3 — First-3-seconds framework · HOOKS

**On-screen hook:** "Your ads are dying in the first 3 seconds."
**B-roll:** rigging / adjusting a light on set, bright, hands working. Or scrubbing an ad timeline on a bright monitor.
**Audio:** trending (↑).

**Caption:**

Save this before you brief your next ad. The 3 ways brands blow the first 3 seconds, and the fix for each 👇

If your ad is getting skipped, it died at the open. Almost always one of these:

1. You opened on the product. Nobody cares about your product yet. Open on their problem instead.
2. You didn't name who it's for. A vague ad talks to everyone and stops no one. Call out the exact person in the first line.
3. You gave no reason to stay. No tension, no question, no loop. Give them something they need the answer to.

Product, no person, no tension. Fix those three and your skip rate drops on its own.

## 4 — Static ads framework · HOOKS

**On-screen hook:** "Your static ads look expensive and sell nothing."
**B-roll:** designing / reviewing a static on a bright screen, or a product shot being set up on set. Movement.
**Audio:** trending (↑).

**Caption:**

Save this before you brief your next static. The 3 things it needs to survive the first glance 👇

A static ad gets less than a second. Most brands spend that second on a logo and a pretty product shot. Wasted.

The ones that convert:

1. One message, readable in a second. If I have to study it, I've already scrolled. Lead with the claim, not the branding.
2. One idea. Not five benefits fighting for space. The more you cram in, the less lands.
3. Looks like content, not a billboard. The second it reads as "an ad", the guard goes up.

Design second. Message first.

## 5 — Creative fatigue framework · HOOKS

**On-screen hook:** "The ad carrying your whole account is already dying."
**B-roll:** walking through the studio, or reviewing dashboards/numbers on a bright screen. Movement, not static.
**Audio:** trending (↑).

**Caption:**

Save this. 3 signs your best ad is about to tank, and what to do before it does 👇

Every winning ad fatigues. Not because it got worse, but because your audience saw it too many times. The trap is waiting for it to die, then panicking. The signs show up early if you know where to look:

1. Frequency climbing. The same people are seeing it 4, 5, 6 times.
2. CTR sliding while spend holds. They're still being shown it. They've just stopped caring.
3. CPA creeping up week on week. The winner is quietly getting expensive.

The fix isn't a better ad. It's the next one ready before this one drops. You're not chasing one winner. You're building a pipeline.
