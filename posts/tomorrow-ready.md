## 1. Creative brief (Pillar 2, insider) · HOOKS

**On-screen hook:** "Your UGC creator isn't the problem. Your brief is."
**B-roll:** on set directing a creator, handing over notes, movement.
**Audio:** trending (↑).

**Caption:**

Save this before you brief your next creator 👇

Brands blame the creator when the ad flops. Nine times out of ten the creator delivered exactly what the brief asked for. The brief was just vague.

A brief that converts gives them three things:

1. The one job of the ad. Not "talk about the product". The single feeling or problem this specific ad is built around.
2. The hook, written for them. Don't leave the first line to chance. It's the whole ad. Hand it over.
3. A reference that already worked. Show them the exact energy you want. "Make it like this" beats three paragraphs of adjectives.

A good creator with a bad brief makes a bad ad. Every time.

## 2. Ad account audit (Pillar 1) · soft CTA

**On-screen hook:** "The first thing I check in a broken ad account isn't the targeting."
**B-roll:** at the desk reviewing dashboards on a bright screen, pointing at numbers, movement.
**Audio:** trending (↑).

**Caption:**

Save this. The 3 things I check first in a struggling ad account, in order 👇

When someone hands me an account that's stopped working, everyone expects me to dive into audiences and budgets. I don't. I go straight to the creative.

1. Hook rate. What percent make it past 3 seconds? If that's low, nothing downstream matters. The ad's dead on arrival.
2. Creative variety. Ten versions of the same ad, or ten different angles? The same idea on repeat isn't testing.
3. The winning ad's age. If one ad's carried the account for months, it's fatiguing and there's no replacement coming.

The account isn't broken. The creative is. It almost always is.

## 3. Awareness levels (Pillar 1) · HOOKS

**On-screen hook:** "You're pitching people who've never heard of you like they're ready to buy."
**B-roll:** at a whiteboard mapping it out, or writing at the desk, movement.
**Audio:** trending (↑).

**Caption:**

Save this before your next campaign 👇

Most ads make the same mistake. They talk to a cold stranger the same way they'd talk to someone already holding their card. Different people, different message.

Match the ad to how aware they actually are:

1. Never heard of you. Lead with the problem, not the product. Earn the attention first.
2. Knows the problem, not the fix. Show them the mechanism. Why your way works.
3. Comparing options. Now you can talk features, proof, reviews. They're finally ready for it.

Sell to a cold audience like they're warm and you'll lose both.

## 5. Creative mix and plateau (Pillar 1) · HOOKS

**On-screen hook:** "You're running all video ads. That's why you've plateaued."
**B-roll:** reviewing a wall/screen of mixed ads (statics + video), movement.
**Audio:** trending (↑).

**Caption:**

Save this before your next creative round 👇

Brands find one format that works and run it into the ground. All video. All the same. Then growth flatlines and they blame the budget.

The algorithm wants variety. Give it a mix:

1. Video. Your workhorse. Story, demo, UGC.
2. Statics. Cheap and fast to make. They catch the people video misses, often at your best CPA.
3. Different angles inside each. Same product, a different reason to care.

One format is one door into your brand. A mix opens more of them.
