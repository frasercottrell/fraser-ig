## E — Static ads framework (Pillar 1)

**On-screen hook:** "Your static ads look expensive and sell nothing."

**Caption:**

Save this before you brief your next static — the 3 things it needs to survive the first glance 👇

A static ad gets less than a second. Most brands spend that second on a logo and a pretty product shot. Wasted.

The ones that convert:

1. One message, readable in a second. If I have to study it, I've already scrolled. Lead with the claim, not the branding.
2. One idea. Not five benefits fighting for space. The more you cram in, the less lands.
3. Looks like content, not a billboard. The second it reads as "an ad", the guard goes up.

Design second. Message first.

## F — Ad testing framework (Pillar 1) · soft CTA

**On-screen hook:** "You're testing 20 ads a month and learning nothing."

**Caption:**

Save this — how to test ads so every round actually teaches you something 👇

Most "testing" is throwing ads at the wall and watching the numbers move. You get a winner, you can't say why, and next month you start from zero.

Real testing:

1. Change one thing at a time. Same ad, new hook. Now you know it was the hook. Change five things and you've learned nothing.
2. Test big swings, not tweaks. A new angle teaches you more than a new font. Stop A/B-ing button colours.
3. Kill losers fast, scale winners faster. Sitting on a dead ad "to be sure" is just burning budget.
4. Write down what each test taught you. No note, no learning.

Testing without a lesson is just spending.

## G — UGC framework (Pillar 1)

**On-screen hook:** "Your UGC looks like an ad. That's why people scroll past it."

**Caption:**

Save this before your next UGC brief — the 3 things that make it feel real instead of scripted 👇

The whole point of UGC is that it doesn't look like advertising. The second it does, you've lost. Most brands brief it like a polished ad and wonder why it flops.

What actually works:

1. Open like a person, not a brand. No "introducing". Start mid-thought, like they're telling a mate.
2. A real problem in the first line. The thing they actually googled at 1am, not a feature.
3. Filmed like their content, not yours. Shot on a phone, in a real room. Polish kills it.

Brief it like a person talking, not a brand selling.

## H — Iteration framework (Pillar 2)

**On-screen hook:** "Your best ad has 10 more winners hiding inside it. You're leaving them there."

**Caption:**

Save this — how we get 10 ads out of one winner instead of starting from scratch 👇

When an ad wins, most brands celebrate and move on to invent the next one from nothing. Waste. The winner already told you what works. Mine it.

1. Same script, new hook. The body converts — just swap the first 3 seconds and test again.
2. Same hook, new format. Turn the winning video into a static. Into a UGC cut. Same idea, new surface.
3. Same angle, new face. New creator, new visual, the core message that already worked.

You're not out of ideas. You're sitting on a winner you've barely touched.

## I — Meta / broad-targeting framework (Pillar 1) · from the Andromeda topic

**On-screen hook:** "Meta changed the game. Your account is still playing the old one."

**Caption:**

Save this — 3 things to change now the algorithm does your targeting for you 👇

Meta's algorithm got good enough to find your buyer on its own. Most accounts are still built like it's 2019 — stacked audiences, tight interests, exclusions everywhere. You're fighting the machine instead of feeding it.

1. Go broad. Stop slicing audiences into tiny segments. Let it find them.
2. Move the effort to creative. The lever now is how many angles you give it, not how you set up the audience.
3. Feed it variety, not restrictions. More hooks and angles, not more exclusions.

The targeting is solved. The creative isn't. That's where your time goes now.
