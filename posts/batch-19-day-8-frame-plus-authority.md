## Reel 8.1 - F3 authority (credential + reset)
**On-screen hook:** "I've made 10,000 ads. If I started over tomorrow, I'd only use these 5 hooks."
**B-roll:** TIGHT workbench, hands on the camera/gear, fill frame one. No wide floor.

**Caption:**

Save this before you write your next ad 👇

10,000 ads in, if I lost everything and had to start from scratch tomorrow, I wouldn't reinvent anything. I'd write these 5 hooks on repeat until one caught. Steal all of them:

1. The callout. "[Exact person], you're making this mistake." Names who it's for in the first second, so the right viewer feels personally caught. "Founders spending £50k a month on ads, this is for you." Precision is what stops the scroll.

2. The result-and-time. "I did X for 30 days. Here's what happened." A specific action, a specific window, and a payoff you hold back. The specificity reads as real, the open loop keeps them watching.

3. The contrarian. "Everyone tells you X. It's wrong." You pick a fight with what the viewer already believes, and now they have to know why. Tension buys attention nothing else can.

4. The confession. "The most expensive mistake I ever made cost me thousands." Admitting a loss lowers the guard. People trust the person owning a failure more than the one selling a win.

5. The reveal. "The one thing every winning ad has in common." Name a pattern, hide the answer, and the only way to close the loop is to keep watching. Curiosity does the work for you.

Every one opens on the viewer, never the product. That's the whole secret.

## Reel 8.2 - F3 authority (analysis + pattern reveal)
**On-screen hook:** "I studied the highest-spending ads of the last 3 years. They open 1 of 5 ways."
**B-roll:** TIGHT workbench, hands scrolling ads on a screen / flipping frames. Fill frame one.

**Caption:**

Save this before you write your next opening line 👇

I went through the ads that spent the most and lasted the longest over 3 years. The products were all different. The openings weren't. Almost every one started in one of these 5 ways:

1. The question they can't say no to. "Still waking up tired no matter how early you sleep?" A yes-question aimed so precisely the right person nods before they've decided to watch. The nod is the hook.

2. The number drop. "3 things killing your ads that you'd never guess." A specific count plus a threat plus curiosity. Numbers promise a payoff that's easy to picture, so people stay for it.

3. The direct "you". "You're spending money on ads that were dead in the first 3 seconds." Second person, present tense, their exact situation. It feels like you're talking to one person because you are.

4. The mid-story drop-in. Opening like you caught them halfway through a thought. "So this ad just did £2M and it took 20 minutes to shoot." No setup, straight into the interesting part.

5. The bold claim. "Your targeting isn't the problem. It never was." A confident statement that challenges a belief, forcing a "wait, what?" that carries them past the swipe.

None of them open on the product. They open on the person, the problem, or a claim. Then they earn the rest.

## Reel 8.3 - F3 authority (reviewed thousands + mistakes)
**On-screen hook:** "I've reviewed thousands of ad hooks. These 5 mistakes kill most of them."
**B-roll:** TIGHT workbench, hands on gear / a hook doc on screen. Fill frame one.

**Caption:**

Save this before you write your next hook 👇

I've read more ad hooks than almost anyone alive, and the weak ones fail the same 5 ways every time. Check yours against this before you post:

1. It opens on the product. "Introducing our new..." Nobody cares about your product in second one. Open on the problem it solves and earn the right to mention it later.

2. It's too broad. "Struggling with your skin?" talks to everyone and stops no one. "Still breaking out at 30?" stops the exact person. Narrow it until it feels almost too specific. That's where it starts working.

3. It gives the answer away. If the hook tells them the whole point, there's no reason to keep watching. Hold something back. Open the loop, don't close it in the first line.

4. It sounds like an ad. Slogans, brand voice, buzzwords. The second it reads like marketing, the thumb moves. Write it the way a real person actually talks about the problem.

5. It's about you, not them. "We're excited to launch..." No "we". Every strong hook is about the viewer, their problem, their words, their world. If your first line has "we" or "our" in it, rewrite it.

Fix these five and your hook rate climbs before you touch anything else.
