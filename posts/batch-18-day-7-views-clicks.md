## Reel 7.1 - F1 Contrarian
**On-screen hook:** "The first thing I write in an ad isn't the hook."
**B-roll:** tight/busy workbench, hands on gear or a notebook. Arresting first frame, fill it.

**Caption:**

Save this before you write your next ad 👇

Everyone starts with the hook. I don't. If you write the hook first, you're guessing what'll grab people. Here's what I write before it, so the hook writes itself:

1. The customer's exact words. I open their reviews and DMs and copy the phrases they actually use for the problem. "It doesn't feel like anything's on my skin" beats anything I'd invent. The hook is already in there.

2. The one problem. Not five. The single pain that keeps them up. Every strong ad is about one thing, so I pick it before I write a word of the script.

3. The moment it bites. When does that problem actually hurt? 3pm slump, Sunday-night dread, the mirror before a night out. Naming the moment makes the ad feel personal.

4. What they've already tried. If I know what failed them before, I can open with "you've tried X and still..." which instantly says I get it. That earns the watch.

5. THEN the hook. By now the first line is obvious, because it's just their problem, in their words, at the moment it hurts. The hook was never the hard part. The research was.

Write the research first. The hook falls out of it.

## Reel 7.2 - F2 Competitive/FOMO
**On-screen hook:** "Your competitor's ads are boring. That's exactly why they're winning."
**B-roll:** tight/busy workbench, hands assembling gear / two ads on a screen. Arresting first frame.

**Caption:**

Save this before you make another "creative" ad 👇

Go look at the brand outspending you. Their ads are probably plain, simple, almost dull. And that's the point. Here's why boring beats clever in paid social:

1. Boring looks like content. Plain, native, unpolished. The brain doesn't flag it as an ad, so people watch instead of scrolling. Your slick, clever ad screams "advert" in the first frame and gets skipped.

2. Boring is clear. One message, said plainly. Clever ads make people work to get the point, and a distracted scroller won't. Clarity converts. Wit doesn't.

3. Boring is repeatable. A simple format can be remade 20 times fast. A clever concept is a one-off you can't scale. Your competitor ships volume because their template is boring on purpose.

4. Boring ages well. A clever, trendy ad dies when the trend does. A plain "here's the problem, here's the fix" ad works this year, next year, forever. It never gets old because it was never trying to be cool.

5. Boring is about them, not you. Clever ads are usually the brand showing off. Boring ads just name the customer's problem and solve it. Customers don't want clever. They want to feel understood.

Stop trying to make art. Make it plain, make it clear, make it about them.

## Reel 7.3 - F3 Pattern/authority
**On-screen hook:** "I ranked my 10 best-performing ads ever. They all opened the same way."
**B-roll:** tight/busy workbench, hands scrolling ranked ads / flipping frames. Arresting first frame.

**Caption:**

Save this before you write your next opening line 👇

I pulled my 10 highest-performing ads of all time and lined up their first 3 seconds. Different products, different years, different budgets. The openings were almost identical. Here's the pattern:

1. They named a problem, not the product. Every single one opened on something the viewer already felt, before the brand was ever mentioned. Not one led with the product. The problem is the door in.

2. They got specific fast. Not "struggling with your skin" but "still breaking out at 30." The specific version makes the right person feel personally called out, and specificity reads as truth.

3. They used a real voice. First person, plain, the way someone actually talks. No ad-voice, no slogans. It sounded like a person, so people listened like it was one.

4. They opened a loop. A question or a claim that needed an answer, so you couldn't leave. "Here's what nobody tells you about..." The loop carried them past the swipe point.

5. They held the product back. It never showed up in the first 3 seconds. Only once the viewer cared did the product appear, as the answer, not the intro.

Problem, specific, human, open loop, delay the product. That's the opening on every winner I've made.
