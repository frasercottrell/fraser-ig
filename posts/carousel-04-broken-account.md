## Caption

Save this before your next account audit 👇

When a brand hands me an account that's stopped working, everyone in the room expects me to open the audiences and start moving budget around. I don't touch either. Here's the order I actually go in, and why it's the same order every time:

1. Hook rate first, before anything else. What percentage are still watching after 3 seconds? If that number is bad, nothing downstream matters. The ad is dead on arrival and no budget change, no audience swap and no bid strategy rescues it. Everything below this is wasted effort until the hook works.

2. How genuinely different are the ads? Not how many are running. How many separate ideas are in there. Most struggling accounts have ten ads that are one idea in ten outfits, which means the account has never actually tested anything. It's starved of real options and nobody has noticed because the reporting says ten tests.

3. The winner's age and frequency. If one ad has been carrying results for months, check how often people are seeing it. Once frequency climbs, it's fatiguing, and if there's nothing in the pipeline to replace it the account is heading for a drop that looks sudden but was visible weeks earlier.

4. Where the spend actually sits. Struggling accounts pour almost everything into one tired concept. Healthy ones keep a few genuinely different tests live at all times, so there's always a next winner being found while the current one still works.

5. The offer. Last, deliberately. If the creative is good and people are clicking and still nothing converts, the problem moved downstream and it isn't a creative problem any more. A weak offer kills strong creative, and no amount of testing fixes that.

Nine times out of ten it never gets past step one or two. The account isn't broken. The creative is.
