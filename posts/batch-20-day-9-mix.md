My best ad last month broke every rule I teach.

By every best practice going, it should have flopped. It beat everything we spent real money on that month.

It didn't win despite breaking the rules. It won because it felt like a real person talking, not a brand ticking boxes.

## Reel 9.3 - F3 Authority (FOLLOWS/SAVES play)
**On-screen hook:** "After 10,000 ads, here are the 5 things I check before any ad goes live."
**B-roll:** TIGHT workbench, hands on gear / a checklist on screen. Fill frame one.

**Caption:**

Save this and run every ad through it before you post 👇

10,000 ads in, I don't trust my gut anymore. I trust a checklist. Before anything goes live, it has to pass these 5. If it fails one, it goes back:

1. Does the first line pass the "that's me" test? Read it as the customer. Do they instantly feel seen, or am I talking about myself? If it's about us, our brand, our launch, it fails here and never gets posted.

2. Is the product held back past 3 seconds? If it shows up in the opening, people bail before they care. The problem comes first, the product comes once they're leaning in. No exceptions.

3. One message, or many? I try to delete. If the ad still works with a benefit removed, it wasn't needed. The final cut says one thing so clearly a distracted person gets it in a glance.

4. Does it look like content or an ad? If the first frame screams "advert," the thumb moves. It has to look native, rough, real, like something a person would actually stop on, not skip.

5. Is there a reason to keep watching past 5 seconds? An open loop, a question, a "here's what nobody tells you." If everything's given away up front, there's nothing pulling them through. Always leave a thread hanging.

Five checks, two minutes, and it's saved me from posting more losers than I can count.
