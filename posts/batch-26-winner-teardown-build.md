# Batch 26. TEARDOWN of the winner (reel 041) + hooks built from what actually made it work

## The winner
"I've analysed 10,000 ads. These 5 hooks drove the most sales." - 5,948 views (2.2x old record), + green saves/
shares/comments + 4 follows. Same footage + caption + audio as every variant it beat, so the WORDS did it.

## What it beat (same setup, only the line changed)
- These 5 hooks drove the most purchases across 10,000+ ads
- These 5 hooks generated the highest CTR across 10,000+ ads
- These 5 hooks brought in the cheapest customers across 10,000+ ads
- I've analysed 10,000 ads. These 5 hooks got the most clicks / lowest CPA / highest CTR / most revenue / turned viewers into buyers

## WHY it won - the levers, each evidenced by the comparison

1. **PROOF FIRST beat proof-last.** The winner opens "I've analysed 10,000 ads..." THEN the claim. The losers that led with "These 5 hooks... across 10,000+ ads" buried the proof at the end. Front-loading credibility won. (This is exactly why "lead with social proof" is right - the winner already did it.)
2. **PLAIN OUTCOME beat jargon.** "sales" beat CTR, CPA, clicks, revenue, "cheapest customers," even "purchases." The universal, felt, everyone-wants-it word won. Metric jargon narrows the audience and cools it.
3. **ENUMERATED, FINITE PROMISE.** "These 5 hooks" = a countable, get-able, saveable list. A specific small number reads as a complete thing you can have.
4. **OPEN LOOP.** Which 5? You have to watch to find out. The payoff is withheld.
5. **DEAD-ON ICP RELEVANCE.** "hooks that drove sales" is the exact thing an ad-runner wants. No translation needed.
6. **(Constants that enabled it):** tight arresting first frame = the stop; short caption delivering the 5 = the save; trending audio = distribution.

## The transferable formula
[PROOF, first] + [enumerated finite promise: "these N ..."] + [PLAIN high-value outcome] + [open loop].
Most losing variants dropped one ingredient (jargon outcome, or proof-last, or no number). Winners keep ALL of them.
