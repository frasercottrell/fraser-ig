## 1. Creative, not budget (Pillar 1) · HOOKS

**On-screen hook:** "You don't have a spend problem. You have a creative problem."
**B-roll (arresting first frame):** POV holding the camera-rig monitor with an ad playing on the screen (the proven winner). Tight, legible, "what's that".
**Audio:** trending (↑).

**Caption:**

Save this before you ask for more budget 👇

Everyone thinks the fix is more spend. Double the budget, double the sales. Then they double it and nothing moves, because the money was never the problem.

Three signs it's your creative, not your budget:

1. Your CPA climbs the second you scale. Good creative holds as spend goes up. Weak creative only worked because the audience was small and cheap.
2. One ad carries everything. If 90% of results come from one tired ad, that's not a budget problem. It's a creative-volume problem.
3. Your hook rate is low. If people don't stop, more money just buys more people scrolling past.

Budget amplifies what's working. If nothing's working, it just amplifies the leak.

## 2. Boring, not long (Pillar 1) · HOOKS

**On-screen hook:** "Nobody skips your ad because it's too long. They skip because it's boring."
**B-roll (arresting first frame):** an odd, mid-action moment on set (paddling-pool energy). Something you have to look at.
**Audio:** trending (↑).

**Caption:**

Save this before you cut your ad down to 15 seconds 👇

Brands panic about length. "Make it shorter, attention spans are dead." Attention spans are fine. People watch 3-hour podcasts. They just won't give you 3 boring seconds.

What actually keeps them watching:

1. A hook that opens a loop. Ask a question their brain needs answered and they can't leave until you close it.
2. Something new every few seconds. A new visual, a new line, a turn. The second it feels predictable, they're gone.
3. Tension, not information. Don't explain. Make them curious, then make them wait.

Length was never the problem. Boring is. Fix that and you can hold them as long as you want.

## 3. AI for customer research (Pillar 2, AI) · HOOKS

**On-screen hook:** "You're guessing what your customer wants. AI already knows."
**B-roll (arresting first frame):** tight over-the-shoulder at a laptop, AI generating on a glowing screen. Or an unusual AI-shoot setup.
**Audio:** trending (↑).

**Caption:**

Save this before you write your next ad 👇

Most brands write ads based on what they think the customer cares about. It's a guess. Usually a wrong one.

Here's how we use AI to stop guessing:

1. Feed it your reviews. Paste every review, good and bad, and ask what people actually love and hate. The patterns you're too close to see.
2. Ask it to argue against you. "Why wouldn't someone buy this?" The objections it lists are the ones killing your ads.
3. Have it map the exact words they use. Then you write in their language, not your brand's.

AI won't write the ad for you. But it'll hand you the insight most brands never dig for.

## 4. Ugly wins (Pillar 1) · HOOKS

**On-screen hook:** "The best-performing ad in your account is probably the ugliest one."
**B-roll (arresting first frame):** tight contrast shot, a big cinema camera next to a phone propped up filming. Slightly odd, clear focal point.
**Audio:** trending (↑).

**Caption:**

Save this before you spend £10k on a "premium" ad 👇

The polished, cinematic, expensive-looking ad screams advertisement. And people scroll ads. The one that looks like a real person filmed it on their phone? That one gets trusted.

Why ugly wins:

1. It reads as honest. Before a word is said, low production says "this isn't trying to sell me".
2. It looks native. It blends into the feed instead of interrupting it, so the guard stays down.
3. It's fast and cheap to test. 20 raw ads for the cost of one polished one, and you find the winner faster.

Save the cinema camera for the brand film. For performance, real and boring beats beautiful and ignored.

## 5. Scale vs stall (Pillar 1) · HOOKS

**On-screen hook:** "Brands that scale and brands that stall do one thing differently."
**B-roll (arresting first frame):** a striking, unusual studio moment, crew mid-shot rigging something odd. Tight, curious.
**Audio:** trending (↑).

**Caption:**

Save this 👇

Same budget. Same product. Same platform. One brand scales past seven figures, the other plateaus and blames the algorithm. The difference isn't money. It's how they treat creative.

What the ones who scale actually do:

1. They treat creative as the growth lever, not an afterthought. Most of their effort goes into the ad, not the ad account.
2. They kill winners on purpose. They build the next ad before the current one fatigues, so there's never a gap.
3. They know why every winner worked. So they can rebuild it again and again, instead of hoping.

Stalling brands chase settings. Scaling brands chase better creative. That's the whole game.
