## 1. "I've studied thousands of winning ads. Here's what they all had in common."

**On-screen hook:** "I've studied thousands of winning ads. Here's what they all had in common."
**B-roll:** tight POV camera-rig monitor with an ad playing, or flipping through printed ad frames.

**Caption:**

Save this before you write your next ad 👇

I've broken down thousands of winning ads. Skincare, supplements, apparel, tech, every niche. They didn't share a format, a length, or a budget. They shared these 5 things. Steal all of them:

1. They opened on the problem, not the product. The first line named something the viewer already felt, before the brand was ever mentioned. "Still breaking out at 30?" beats "Introducing our new serum" every time. If your first line is about you, rewrite it.

2. They talked to one person, not everyone. Not "for people who want better skin" but "for the person who's tried five creams and given up." Narrow it until it feels almost too specific. That's the point where strangers feel seen instead of sold to.

3. They used the customer's own words. The exact phrases came from reviews and DMs, not the brand deck. "It doesn't feel like anything's on my skin" converts better than "lightweight formula", because a real person actually said the first one.

4. They earned attention before asking for the sale. No pitch in the first 5 seconds. Hook, give, build a little trust, and the product shows up once you already care. Sell too early and you lose everyone who hadn't decided to listen yet.

5. They kept one message. One idea per ad. The losers crammed in five benefits and landed none. The winners picked the single strongest reason to buy and hammered it. If you're trying to say everything, you're saying nothing.

That's the whole pattern. Not production. Not budget. These five moves, on repeat.

## 2. "The first thing I check in a broken ad account isn't the targeting."

**On-screen hook:** "The first thing I check in a broken ad account isn't the targeting."
**B-roll:** tight POV camera-rig monitor / reviewing dashboards on a bright screen.

**Caption:**

Save this before your next account audit 👇

When a brand hands me an account that's stopped working, everyone expects me to dive into audiences and budgets. I don't touch them. Here's the exact order I go in, every time:

1. Hook rate. What percent make it past 3 seconds? If it's under 30%, nothing downstream matters. The ad's dead on arrival and no budget change saves it. This is always where I start.

2. Creative variety. Ten versions of the same ad, or ten genuinely different angles? If it's the same idea in ten outfits, you're not testing, you're repeating. The account is starved of real options.

3. The winner's age and frequency. If one ad has carried results for months, check its frequency. Above 3–4 and it's fatiguing. If there's no replacement in the pipeline, the account is about to fall off a cliff nobody saw coming.

4. Ratio of spend to angles. Most struggling accounts are pouring 90% of budget into one tired concept. Healthy accounts spread across a few live tests so there's always a next winner being found.

5. The offer itself. Last, not first. If the ads are fine but nothing converts, the problem moved downstream. A boring offer kills great creative, and no amount of targeting fixes that.

Nine times out of ten, the account isn't broken. The creative is. It almost always is.

## 3. "Your ads are dying in the first 3 seconds. Here are the 5 reasons why."

**On-screen hook:** "Your ads are dying in the first 3 seconds."
**B-roll:** tight, hands scrubbing an ad timeline / rigging a light. Fill frame one.

**Caption:**

Save this before you brief your next ad 👇

If your ad is getting skipped, it died at the open. Not the edit, not the offer, the first 3 seconds. Here are the 5 ways brands blow it, and the fix for each:

1. You opened on the product. Nobody cares about your product yet. Open on the problem it solves and earn the attention first. Product comes after they care.

2. You didn't name who it's for. A vague ad talks to everyone and stops no one. Call out the exact person in the first line so the right viewer feels the jolt of "that's me".

3. You led with a feature, not a feeling. "30% more vitamin C" is a spec. "The tired-skin fix I wish I'd found sooner" is a feeling. People buy the feeling, then justify with the feature.

4. There was no reason to keep watching. You told them everything up front, so they left. Open a loop. A question, a claim, a "here's what nobody tells you" they now need answered.

5. The first frame was boring to look at. Even a perfect line dies over a dull visual. Open on movement, or something slightly odd, or a tight shot that makes the thumb hesitate for half a second. That half-second is the whole game.

Fix those five and your hook rate climbs on its own, no budget change required.

## 4. "Steal these 5 ad hooks. They work in almost any niche."

**On-screen hook:** "Steal these 5 ad hooks. They work in almost any niche."
**B-roll:** tight, typing / writing hooks on a bright screen. Movement.

**Caption:**

Save this before you write your next 10 ads 👇

You don't need to invent hooks from scratch. These 5 formats have printed money across dozens of accounts. Swap in your product and your customer's problem:

1. The callout. "[Specific person], you're making this [specific] mistake." Names exactly who it's for and hits a nerve. "DTC founders spending £50k a month, this one's for you."

2. The mistake. "Stop doing [common thing]. Here's what to do instead." People are wired to check they're not the one getting it wrong. Works because it promises to save them from looking stupid.

3. The number. "I tried [X] for 30 days. Here's what happened." A specific number plus a time frame plus a payoff you're withholding. The specificity is what makes it feel real.

4. The contrarian. "Everyone tells you [common advice]. It's wrong." Pattern interrupt. It stops the scroll because it argues with what the viewer already believes, and now they have to know why.

5. The reveal. "The one thing every [winning thing] has in common." Curiosity gap. You've named a pattern and hidden the answer, and the only way to close the loop is to keep watching.

Rotate these across your next batch and watch which one your audience responds to. Then double down on the winner.

## 5. "5 signs your best ad is about to die (and how to catch it early)."

**On-screen hook:** "5 signs your best ad is about to die."
**B-roll:** tight, reviewing numbers on a bright screen. Movement, not static.

**Caption:**

Save this and check your account against it this week 👇

Every winning ad dies eventually. The brands that scale are the ones who see it coming and have the next one ready. Here are the 5 early warning signs:

1. Frequency climbing past 3. The same people are seeing it 4, 5, 6 times. They've stopped noticing it. The ad didn't get worse, the audience got tired.

2. CTR sliding while spend holds. It's still being shown as much, people just aren't clicking like they were. That's fatigue showing up before the CPA does.

3. CPA creeping up week on week. Slow, quiet, easy to ignore for a month. By the time it's obvious, you've already burned budget you didn't need to.

4. Comments drying up. Early on a winner gets saves, shares, comments. When the engagement goes quiet, the novelty's gone even if the numbers look ok for now.

5. You're bored of it. Genuinely the most reliable signal, and the one founders ignore. If you're sick of seeing it, your audience saw it first. That's your cue to launch the next one, not to kill this one out of boredom.

The fix is never a better single ad. It's having the next one ready before this one drops, so there's never a gap. You're not chasing one winner. You're building a pipeline.
