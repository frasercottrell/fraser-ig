# Examples — templated vs. in-voice

The same talking-head video (the "3 static ad formats that convert" one) captioned two ways. Both are
technically correct. The second is the standard. Study the difference.

---

## ❌ Templated (avoid) — reads like a machine filled a form

> Save this before you make another static ad 👇
>
> I've tested thousands of static ads. Most of them flop. But three formats keep converting no matter
> the brand or the product, and between them they can carry a whole account. Here they are, and why
> each one works:
>
> 1. The native story. A UGC-style image, some native looking text... It works because it doesn't look
> like an ad...
> 2. The headline static. A great product image... It works because it does one job clearly...
> 3. The review static. Copy and paste your best review... This one works because it isn't you claiming
> you're good...
>
> Follow @frasercottrell for more of what actually converts.
> Comment STATIC and I'll send you a swipe file of my favourite static ads.

**Why it's weak:** the default "Save this before X 👇" opener, every point the same "It works because…"
shape, no credential, no personality, a bare CTA. Correct but forgettable.

## ✅ In-voice (the standard) — credential opener, personality, question + keyword CTA

> I've made over 10,000 ads. Thousands of them were statics, and honestly most of them flopped.
>
> But three formats just kept winning, over and over, no matter the brand. If you've got these three
> running, you're basically covered.
>
> 1. The native story. A UGC-style photo, some native looking text, bonus points for an "ask me
> anything" box slapped on top. The whole point is that it doesn't look like an ad. It sits in the
> feed like a normal post and people actually read it. Make it look like content, not a billboard.
>
> 2. The headline static. One strong product image (you can generate a great one with AI in minutes
> now), one catchy headline, maybe a subline. That's it. Its only job is to be clear. Most brands ruin
> it by cramming five benefits in. Pick the one that matters and say it BIG.
>
> 3. The review static. Copy your best review, paste it onto a product image, done. It works because
> it's not you saying you're good, it's a customer saying it. Your reviews are a goldmine and barely
> anyone raids them. The copy's already written for you. Go steal it.
>
> None of this needs a shoot, a designer, or a budget. A photo, a product image, a screenshot. If all
> three are live in your account you're hitting the three reasons people actually buy: it feels native,
> it's clear, and it's trusted.
>
> Which one are you missing right now? 👇
>
> Comment STATIC and I'll send you a swipe file of my favourite static ads.

**Why it works:** opens on the credential/number instead of a driver line; personality ("slapped on
top", "say it BIG", "go steal it", "barely anyone raids them"); closes on a question that pulls comments
*then* the aligned keyword (STATIC → static swipe, not HOOKS). Same information, reads like Fraser.

## Mode quick-reference

- **Micro:** a tool tip or single point → 1–2 lines + one CTA. Don't force a listicle.
- **Story / opinion:** a founder hot take → short punchy paragraphs, one belief, land it hard.
- **Value listicle:** a framework/teardown → numbered points each with the WHY + a closing "the real
  lesson is bigger than this" frame.

All three: rotate the opener, weave a credential, keep it em-dash-free, run voice-lint, align the freebie.
