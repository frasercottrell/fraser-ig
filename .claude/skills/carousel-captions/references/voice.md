# Fraser's Voice, for Captions

Two layers. The linter strips the AI surface. This file supplies the voice. **Both are needed, and
the second one is the harder half:** a draft that passes the lint but was written without this corpus
comes out clean and generic, offends nobody, and sounds like nobody. That's the residual failure mode
and it's worse than a stray flagged word, because generic writing doesn't just read badly on Fraser's
account, it breaks the authority the content exists to build.

The corpus below is real. Every line in the first section is one Fraser actually wrote or rewrote.
Everything in the second section is a line he cut. When this file and a general rule disagree, the
corpus wins.

---

## 1. What he actually sounds like

Real lines, from carousels he wrote or rewrote.

### He hedges, constantly, and it makes him more credible not less

> Those first lines are **probably** already written by your customer
> your best ad is **probably** the one your team is embarrassed by
> That cost has **basically** gone
> The brands out-testing you **usually** aren't outspending you

Absolute claims read as content-marketing confidence. The hedge reads as someone who has actually
thought about it and isn't overselling. When you catch yourself asserting flat, soften it.

### He uses vague-on-purpose words in the connective tissue

> You are just testing **the same stuff**, adding no diversity to your account
> "if you're on a GLP1" **so simple but it works**

Nobody would write "stuff" in a deck. Everybody says it. The casual imprecision between the precise
bits is what makes the precise bits read as real.

### He gives instructions that send them into their own account

> **go back and check your reports.** You launched ten ads last month. 9 of them were the same ads.
> **go and look at your top performer right now.** I bet it's not the one you were proud of

An imperative pulls someone into their own situation mid-read. Far stronger than describing them to
themselves.

### He names the object literally in front of them

> So while you're still staring at **a blank google doc**, they've already tested 10 new angles

An earlier draft said "while you're still waiting on one shoot", which describes a process. His names
the thing on their desk at that exact moment. When you need a concrete image, reach for the object,
not the process.

### He puts the aside after the claim, never before

> I scaled a brand 70% with photos of people's feet. **For real.**
> I've made over 10,000 ads. The ones that made the most money... looked the worst.
> Your competitors are probably testing 10x more ads than you. **I know how that sounds.**

The punch lands first, then the human beat. A softener is fine *if it sounds like him*:
**"Hate to break it to you but"** works. "Not to be dramatic but", "Let me be real", "In today's
landscape" do not. The test is whether the line could top anyone's post.

Note: with a softener up front he drops the hedge. "Hate to break it to you but your competitor
**is** testing 10x more ads." Softener or hedge, not both, or it reads apologetic.

### He uses account vocabulary without explaining it

> Changing the **TOS** isn't a good enough test
> The next one is a **UGC yapper**
> adding no **diversity** to your account
> help write the angles and pump out **net new ideas**

The audience already runs ads. Insider shorthand qualifies the reader in rather than shutting them
out. Getting the platform's vocabulary *wrong* is its own tell: "thumbnail" and "edit" are YouTube
words, and he swapped them for "headline" and "first line in the hook".

### He closes on permission or an image, not on homework

> **Just because it worked once, doesn't mean that's the only ad you should be running.**
> You don't need a better camera, you need a better first line.

He cut a three-line process instruction ("write the angles first, on paper, kill the duplicates") and
replaced it with one line of permission. He also cut a closing statement entirely when the slide
before it was already a strong image. An image that lands the point is a valid close on its own.

### His CTAs are gifts, and cheeky

> **If you want to cheat, i made you 34 top hooks**, comment HOOK
> Want to see exactly how we did it? Comment FEET and **I'll send you the link**
> Want my video on scripting great ads with the help of AI? Comment 'SCRIPT' and **I'll DM it to you**

"i made you" not "the ones I use". The delivery is part of the offer. And the framing has a bit of
mischief in it, which is straight out of the voice doc's "confident, slightly cheeky".

### Mechanics he actually types

- **Digits, not words.** "9 of them", "10x", "34", "70%".
- **Mid-line "..." before a reversal.** "the same ad... ten times", "you'd think no one wanted to see
  dry skin... we were wrong". Not a generic pause. It sits immediately before a turn.
- **Notation.** "To most people polish = Ad". An equals sign beats a clause.
- **Lowercase line starts and dropped full stops.** "it's not going to teach you anything".
- **Comma splices.** "i made you 34 top hooks, comment HOOK".

In a caption these read as fast typing, which is the register. Don't sand them out.

## 2. What he cuts (negative corpus)

Every line below was written by Claude, sounded good, and got deleted. This is the most useful
section in the file, because these are specifically the things a competent model writes that Fraser
doesn't.

### Clever constructions

| Cut | Why |
|---|---|
| "Meta doesn't punish you for it. People do" | Chiasmus. Built for rhythm. |
| "not with targeting. with words" | Contrast built for rhythm. |
| "beats any lookalike you can build" | Comparative flourish. |
| "The best lines aren't written, they're stolen" | not-X-but-Y used decoratively. |
| "that's the one that scaled" | Demonstrative-plus-fragment. Nobody talks like that. |

The test is not "is this a summary or a consequence". It's **would this line survive if it weren't
also satisfying to have written.** Clever is the tell.

Note the negation rule is narrower than it looks. Fraser writes negation constantly when it denies
something the reader actually believes: "You're not wasting money on ads", "It's never the targeting",
"It isn't budget and it isn't effort". That's doing work. It's a tell only when it's there for rhythm.

### Setups

He deletes the build-up line and opens with the punch instead.

> Mine: "The best lines aren't written, they're stolen / reviews, DMs, the comments…"
> His: "Those first lines are probably already written by your customer / reviews, DMs, the comments…"

**If a line exists to make the next line land, cut it and let the next line open.** The exception is a
story, where the build-up is the value, not decoration.

### Punchier over plainer

> Mine: "that's the one that scaled" (5 words)
> His: "it ended up being the best performing ad we'd made." (10 words)

He went **longer to be plainer**. Compression is a side effect of plain speech, not the target. When
the short version is the constructed one, the long plain version wins.

### Abstractions where a move belongs

> Mine: "The recognition is what sold it"
> His: "We mirrored people's problem in the hook"

Name the technique someone could do on Monday, in account vocabulary. Abstractions are for the
caption's framing block at most, never as the payload.

### Invented detail. This is the one that keeps happening.

Cut across three separate rewrites: "bloating at 3pm", "4,000 reviews", "the client hated every
single one of them", "every ad we'd made looked like a pharmacy advert", "close up, bad lighting".

None of it was true. It was generated to satisfy a specificity rule, and he removed all of it every
time.

**If you weren't told it, you don't know it.** Covers numbers, client reactions, production details,
dates, room dynamics. Where the detail isn't available, either go plain or use a device that asserts
nothing: *"you'd think no one wanted to see dry skin... we were wrong"* costs nothing factually and
does the work the invented detail was trying to do.

### Banned outright

Em-dashes. "Here's the thing", "The result?", "Sound familiar?". Balanced triads. "It's not just X,
it's Y". "Unlock", "leverage", "supercharge", "elevate", "game-changer". Participle tails
("…, ensuring your ads convert"). "Experts agree". Exclamation marks for fake enthusiasm. Decorative
emoji strings.

## 3. The check

1. **Draft it.**
2. **Run the bundled linter.** It's at `scripts/voice-lint.py` inside this skill, so it works wherever
   the skill lives:
   `python3 <skill-dir>/scripts/voice-lint.py draft.txt` (or pipe with `-`)
   It's deterministic and cannot be argued with, which is the point.
3. **Fix flags by rewriting in his register, not by thesaurus-swapping.** Swapping "elevate" for
   "enhance" trades one tell for another. Go back to section 1 and say the plain thing the way he says
   it.
4. **Then the judgment pass, which the linter can't do.** Read the draft against section 1 and ask:
   does this sound like the person who wrote "go and look at your top performer right now" and
   "To most people polish = Ad"? Or does it sound like a competent marketer?

**A clean lint is not the goal.** It only proves the AI surface is gone. If it passes and still sounds
like anybody, the problem is that you wrote it without the corpus, and the fix is more of section 1,
not more scrubbing.
