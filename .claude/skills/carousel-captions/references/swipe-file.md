# Carousel Caption Swipe File

Real captions collected from working creators, from several different accounts. Fraser supplied these
as the reference set the carousel caption standard was derived from. Spacing in the originals was
inconsistent; the line breaks below are normalised.

**These are references for structure, not voice.** They come from different people with different
personas. Take the shape; the voice comes from `context/tone-of-voice.md`.

## The carousel captions (the ones the template comes from)

### Meta ads changes

> 5 Meta ads changes that broke August.
>
> Swipe all 8 slides before you touch another campaign.
>
> If your delivery, your costs, or your reporting went sideways this month, the reason is probably in here. Every change in this carousel has a source you can read yourself.

The cleanest example of the five-block template. Note what it does not do: it never lists the 5
changes. The carousel does that. Note also "Every change in this carousel has a source you can read
yourself", which is credibility without teaching, and the save prompt naming a specific future moment.

A partial exception worth understanding. It does list the six things, but they're one-line index
entries rather than taught points, and it's a *news* carousel where the list is the news. The CTA is
the strongest in the set: "No link-in-bio hunt" sells the delivery, not just the asset.

Don't treat this as licence to list the framework. When in doubt, follow the Meta-changes shape.

marks are the opposite of Fraser's voice doc. **Structure only, never the register.**

## The long ones, and why they're long

These two run long because the caption **is** the content. They sit under a single image or a short
video with no slides to carry the value, so the caption has to. That's the opposite of a carousel.

Note what they are: **stories with real specifics**, not listicles of advice. The numbered points are
steps in something that happened, each with a concrete detail attached.

### The ManyChat replacement

> Claude Code just killed my $120/mo ManyChat bill.
>
> I asked it to do some research first: has anyone already built an open source version of ManyChat? It found 4 projects, cloned all of them, and took the best pieces to build me my own comment to DM engine.
>
> The wild part is how much it set up on its own:
>
> 1. Logged into the Meta developer portal with the Playwright MCP and created the app for me
> 2. Wired everything into Trigger.dev so it runs in the cloud 24/7
> 3. Rebuilt the follow gate and the delivery buttons, same as my real funnel
>
> Then I tested it live. Commented on one of my reels from another account, and the DM came back: "this DM came from our own engine, not ManyChat."
>
> First 12 hours running for real: 899 comments processed, 224 DMs sent, 0 duplicates. (ManyChat was still running alongside it and raced my engine to a bunch of replies, haha. They can coexist while I migrate.)
>
> So now whenever I post, I just tell Claude Code "whip up a DM automation for this, use this keyword" and off it goes. I'm never dragging boxes around a flow canvas again.
>
> It's running on this exact post. Comment MANYCHAT and I'll DM you the exact instructions to build this yourself :)

What makes it work: exact numbers ($120/mo, 4 projects, 899 comments, 224 DMs, 0 duplicates), a live
proof moment, a parenthetical aside with a "haha" in it, and a CTA that points at the thing the post
is literally running on.

>

Short paragraphs, a named problem ("typing notes under it is miserable"), then the fix. No listicle at
all. Proof that long-form doesn't have to mean numbered.

## Patterns across the whole set

- **Blank line between every block.** One idea per block, always.
- **The swipe instruction is explicit** on the carousel ones, usually with the slide count.
- **The keyword is always a single word in caps**, sometimes in quotes.
- **The CTA sells the delivery**, not only the asset: "I'll DM it to you", "no link-in-bio hunt",
  "straight to your inbox".
- **Casual asides land well.** "haha", ":)", a parenthetical. It reads as a person posting rather than
  a brand publishing.
- **Emoji are functional**, pointing at the swipe or the CTA. The decorative strings come from the one
  example whose voice we're not copying.
