## The one test

Fraser is a practitioner selling authority. The agency ($10k+/mo, brands spending ~$80k+/mo on paid) and the
course (~$900, DIY) are both bought on one belief: "this person understands ad creative better than I do."
Every idea: does it deepen or dilute that belief? The profile test: a stranger lands and thinks
"if anyone understands ad creative, it's Fraser." Every post answers "what can I teach my audience today?"

Funnel: cold stranger > trial reel (cheap hook test) > follow > main-feed talking head (belief) > comment keyword
> freebie (owned email) > course > some graduate to agency. Nice Ads newsletter (Mondays, 3 ad breakdowns, ~12k subs)
already feeds the top. Twitter is ~90% of agency growth; YouTube weekly long-form is strong on AI; IG is the new channel.

## Audience

E-com founders, heads of marketing/growth. ~80% male, US + UK, 18 to 44. Already running Meta/TikTok ads, already know
creative is the problem; the gap is time, skill, or volume. Pitch above beginner. "Never launched an ad" content pulls
the wrong crowd. AI content performs but can pull AI tourists: always frame it for operators ("how a real agency uses
this in the workflow"), never "AI hacks".

## Two formats, two scoreboards (do not mix them)

**Trial reels** (cold non-followers only; 2 to 3 a day). 5 to 7s B-roll + one line of on-screen text + trending audio
+ long save-worthy caption + follow CTA + comment-keyword CTA. Scoreboard: skip rate, watch, follows, saves.
**Main-feed talking heads** (followers + some cold; 2 to 3 a week, 10 to 30s, sometimes up to a minute when reacting
to an ad on screen). Scoreboard: saves, shares, comments. Do NOT judge these on skip rate (Grüns teardown had 66% skip
and was the best talking head). Long talky clips flop as trial reels; that is the format, not the content.
**Carousels** (1 to 3 a week, under-tested): slide 1 stops, slide 2 opens the gap so they must swipe, 3 to 7 deliver,
last slide = matching comment-keyword CTA. Georgia's first three to build: "Most brands are wasting money because
XYZ", "I've tested 10,000+ ads, this is what actually works", one wildcard.

## What the data says (17 reels logged, through ~Aug 20)

Trial reels:
- **First frame decides the stop.** Tight, centred, slightly odd, implies real ad production (camera-rig POV with an ad
  on the Atomos monitor, the shirtless-guy-in-a-pool BTS shot) = 28 to 35% skip. Wide studio shots, dark shots, Fraser
  sitting and thinking = 50 to 64% skip. Single biggest lever, confirmed several times. Fraser-on-camera underperforms
  for cold ads reels; keep him on camera for the main feed and personal stories.
- **Topic drives engagement separately from the stop.** AI (operator-framed) gets the likes/saves/shares/follows.
  Founder opinions stop well and convert nothing. Universal pains (audit, "your ads are dying") expand reach furthest;
  narrow ones ("your UGC creator") stall.
- **Hook order of stopping power:** identity/mirror ("your agency can't tell you why...") > personal story > authority
  framework > abstract aphorism ("the best copywriter is your customer"). Name the viewer and a felt pain. Cut
  "...here's why" and "...." tails. Put the word "ads" in nearly every hook.
- **Saves are the open bottleneck.** Stop and reach are solved; saves sit near zero except on dead-concrete checklists
  (the "3 jobs of a hook" reel: 3 saves, 1 follow, first keyword lead). Hypothesis in play: saves come from
  do-this-now checklists, not "here's why" insights. Captions were moved to a much longer 5-point listicle standard
  for this reason.
- **A contrarian universal hook can rescue weak B-roll** ("Nobody skips your ad because it's too long. They skip
  because it's boring." = 38% skip on a wide shot). Best case is both.
- Best trial reel so far: "The one thing every high-converting ad has in common" (870 reached, 4 saves, 1 share,
  35.6% skip). n=1; the repo is explicit about not crowning a formula off it.

Talking heads:
- **Real-brand teardowns out-share hot takes.** Grüns teardown: 848 views, 7 comments, 3 shares, 3 reposts. Ugly-ads
  hot take: 543 views, 4 saves, 0 shares. Specific + contrarian on-screen text wins ("GRÜNS is doing what 90% of
  wellness brands get wrong" beat the drafted "Everyone missed why this Grüns ad works").

The 3 hook families that have worked (Georgia call 2; test angles inside these instead of inventing new hooks):
1. Contrarian expertise: "The first thing I check in a broken ad account isn't the targeting."
2. Competitive gap / FOMO: "Your competitor is testing 10x more ads than you. They're using AI."
3. Pattern recognition / authority: "The one thing every high-converting ad has in common."
Fill-ins: "Most brands think X. Actually, Y." / "The first thing I check isn't X" / "Your competitor is doing X.
Here's why." / "Every winning X I've seen does Y." / "I analysed X and noticed Y."

Testing discipline: one variable per reel (hook family, angle, format, or topic) and log it by family in results-log.

**Call 3 (Georgia, email pasted 2026-09-16; verbatim in `fraser-ig/context/sources/georgia-call-3-email.md`, NOT in the
GitHub repo yet).** Content is strong; on-screen hooks need curiosity, urgency and FOMO. A hook must make not
watching or not acting feel like it has a cost, not just say what the video is about. Five questions to run every
hook through: why should I care / what do they know that I don't / what am I missing / what is this costing me /
why keep watching. Contextualise a brand when naming it (14,000 ads means nothing until you say most brands are
nowhere near). Pre-post action: rewrite the text hooks on already-filmed reels, footage stays. Carousels: make the
reader feel the cost of getting it wrong, not the tactic. New actionables: 2 to 3 B-roll posts a week on the MAIN feed
(new; B-roll was trial-only before), one story a week with hook + reply-keyword CTA, Q&A box seeded with 5 to 6 ad
questions. Runneth's read: this is the F2 competitive-gap family generalised across all three families, and F2 already
had the best skip (28.4%) and a follow, so the data backs it. Watch-out: Georgia's fake-podcast carousel draft talks
to the viewer as a podcast listener being tricked, not as an operator; reframe to the founder's cost before using it.

## Voice (short version; full in tone-of-voice.md and instagram-voice-patterns.md)

Sharp, cheeky, British practitioner talking to one busy DTC founder like a mate who actually makes the ads. Opens
cold on a claim, number, confession, or threat, "you/your" in fast, never a greeting, never opens with "I" in
writing. Confident about the work, self-deprecating about himself ("or at least I'm trying to"). Recipe cadence
("all you need is..."), sequencing ("first thing... next thing..."), softeners ("essentially", "basically", "kind
of", "I'd say"). His words: yap/yapping, waffle, low-fi, the mad thing, hook, creative, static, B-roll, ad library.
Never: leverage, unlock, elevate, game-changer, "excited to share", exclamation-mark enthusiasm, listicle intros,
em dashes anywhere written. Humour is dry, deadpan, in asides, often gross-out honest (feet pics, shaved dry feet).
Soft close: "hit the follow button" or "comment X and I'll send it over."

## The scoreboards, rebuilt from real numbers (insights batch, 2026-09-16)

Thirteen posts logged with full insights (Fraser's screenshots, 2026-09-16). These replace any earlier rate guesses. Detail in `fraser-ig/context/results-log.md` entries dated 2026-09-16.

**Format jobs (each format has ONE job):**
- Trial reels: cold reach. Judge on skip rate in 48h. Winners graduate to the feed (the 7k 5-hooks reel is the proof case).
- Talking heads: follows, but only from warm viewers, 0.3 to 0.5% of viewers. On a 4 to 6 week tail; never call follows early.
- Carousels: saves + keyword leads. 0 follows across n=3, flat by day 10, read at 2 weeks. Views/viewer 2.2 to 2.5, so compare formats on viewers, never views.
- List-on-screen trend reels: saves. 5.9% record (FORMAT, 44 saves). Watch time drops to 6s because the value is visible; do not judge these on watch.

**The save ladder (save rate on viewers):** formats menu 4.2-5.9% > pattern data 2.9% > contested AI take 2.0% > named tools 1.9% > reasons list 1.7% > client story 1.6% > teardown 1.5% > hot take/principle/argument 1.0%. People save a reusable menu for their desk. "Formats" is the strongest word on the account. "Save this" openers do not create saves; the payload does.

**Follows:** 0.3-0.5% of viewers at 400-700 viewers; 0.075% at 5,311 (4 follows on the 7k reel). Reach is not the follow lever. Everything since Aug 24 converted 0 follows except the FORMAT reel (4) and AI slop (1): the August warm pool is harvested, new follows come from cold arrivals warming up via trial reels. That is the flywheel working.

**Carousel cover rule:** one short sentence that states the finding (896 viewers) vs 3-4 line text boxes (313-478). Face on the cover does not matter. "Here's what you're missing" / "Here's what they know that you don't" = promise tax, marks the losers. State the finding instead.

**Watch time:** 8s average on every talking head 35-48s (n=7); 10s on the 60s AI-slop piece (unconfirmed); 6s on the 68s Ryze piece (weakest). The first 8 seconds is the ad for the rest.

**Keywords:** comments only come from keywords or direct questions (n=6 for "no keyword = no comments"). Promise-strength order: YAP 27 > HOOKS 31 across 2 > SCRIPT 10 > TRANSLATE 8 > FEET 4 > STATIC 1. Stealable process ("framework") beats "swipe file". Insights comment counts include DM triggers; public comments are separate and thin. Saves and keyword comments stack (15+27 on ugly-ads carousel).

**Talking-head recipe (from the account's own numbers):** open on a brand, a number, or a checkable claim; named usable payload in the body; keyword or direct question. Nothing has hit all three plus the under-25s cut yet; that is the next main-feed test.

**Open watch items:** does the 10s hold repeat on example-led reels; does the Ryze reel recover by day 5-7 (if not, recut the other two filmed Ryze reels: under 25s, finding in TOS, keyword); does a carousel with all five ingredients (one-sentence cover, face optional, listicle caption, menu payload, keyword) beat 896 viewers.
