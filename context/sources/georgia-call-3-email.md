Hi Fraser

Key feedback from session 3 call:

The reel content itself is strong, but the on-screen hooks need to create more curiosity, urgency and FOMO.

The strongest hooks don't simply tell the viewer what the video is about, they make the viewer feel that not watching or acting on the information has a cost.

The main questions to ask when writing a hook:
- Why should I care about this?
- What do they know that I don't?
- What am I potentially missing out on?
- What is this costing me if I'm not doing it?
- Why do I need to keep watching?

Ryze example

Current hook: "Ryze has 14,000 ads live, and here's exactly how they do it."
Issue: The viewer doesn't immediately understand why 14,000 ads matters or why they should care.

Consider contextualising the brand when mentioning them.

Stronger directions:
- "Ryze has 14,000+ ads live. Here's what they know that you don't."
- "Ryze has 14,000+ ads live, and most brands aren't even close. Here's why."
- "Ryze is running 14,000+ ads. Here's the system that lets them scale without guessing."

Claude example

Current hook: "This is why you should be using Claude to write your ad scripts…"
The stronger angle is creating a fear of being left behind and highlighting the time/opportunity cost.
Possible directions:
- "I hate to break it to you, but if you're still writing ad scripts manually, you're already behind."
- "You're wasting hours writing ad scripts. Claude Code can do this instead."
- "That ad script that took you 2 hours to write? Claude Code can do it in seconds."

Founder Story Reel
Current: "Here's how you can turn your founder story into your best performing ad."
This is useful but fairly neutral. It needs more urgency, threat or curiosity.
Stronger directions:
- "Your competitors can copy your product. They can't copy your founder story."
- "Founders: stop letting your competitors tell a better story than you."
- "Not sharing your founder story is costing you [X]."

Action point:
Review the text hooks on already-filmed Reels before posting.
The footage doesn't need to change, focus on making the on-screen hook more intriguing, creating a stronger curiosity gap and making the cost of not watching/acting clear.

2. Carousel Content
Key feedback
The carousel concept is good, but currently it explains the tactic rather than making the reader feel why getting it wrong costs them money.

The key question to answer is: "Why should I care if my podcast ad feels fake?"
The carousel should make the reader think about what their competitors are doing and what they could be missing.
Possible stronger hooks:
- "Your competitors are creating fake podcasts to make ads that don't look like ads. Here's what you're missing."
- "Your competitors are turning fake podcast content into an endless stream of ad creatives."
- "Once I realised that I could turn one fake podcast into dozens of ad creatives, everything changed…"

Potential carousel structure
- Slide 1: That podcast clip you watched this morning? It might have been a fake ad tricking you into watching.
- Slide 2: And that "genuine" conversation you thought was someone recommending a product? It could have been created specifically to sell it to you.
- Slide 3: Brands are now using AI to make ads that look like organic podcast content.
- Slide 4: Because you're far more likely to trust a product when it feels like a real conversation…not an ad.
- Slide 5: The scary part? You might not even realise you're watching an ad.
- Slide 6: While you're scrolling past obvious ads, brands are turning podcast-style content into ads designed to feel completely organic.
- Slide 7: The future of ads won't look like ads. And that's exactly why they work.
