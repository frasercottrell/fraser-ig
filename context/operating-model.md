# Operating Model — How to Think About This (not just follow it)

Everything else in `context/` is the *what*. This is the *why* — the reasoning to hold in
your head so you can improvise, adapt, and know when to break the rules. If a rule and this
document ever conflict, reason from here. Georgia's advice is a strong starting model, not scripture.

---

## The one thing everything serves

Fraser is a **practitioner selling authority.** The agency ($10k+/mo) and the course ($900)
are both bought on one belief: *"this person understands ad creative better than I do."* Every
content decision is really answering: **does this deepen or dilute that belief?**

That's the master test. "Post daily," "use hooks," "make it saveable" are all just tactics that
usually serve it. When a tactic *doesn't* serve it in a specific case, the tactic loses.

---

## Why each tactic actually works (so I can flex it)

**Trial reels — the growth engine.**
The mechanism: they test *hooks* against cold non-followers with near-zero production cost, in
secret, so failure is free. The point isn't "post 3 B-roll videos a day" — it's *run cheap
experiments on what makes a stranger stop and follow.* So: reuse B-roll (the B-roll isn't the
variable — the hook is), rotate hooks across pillars (to learn which pillar pulls cold audience),
and read results as data, not vanity. If a different cheap format tests hooks better, that's still
"trial reels" in spirit.

**Well-known brands (Nike/Apple/Gymshark) in Pillar 1.**
The mechanism: borrowed context. A stranger scrolling gives ~1 second. A recognisable brand buys
the stop because they already have an opinion. You're renting their salience to introduce your
expertise. Implication: the *brand* is bait; the *insight* is the payload. A breakdown that's all
brand and no sharp mechanism fails the master test — it entertained, it didn't prove authority.

**Identity → Pain → Curiosity hooks.**
The mechanism: self-selection + open loop. Naming the identity ("DTC founders spending £50k+/mo")
makes the right person feel *seen* and the wrong person scroll on (good — we want the right
follower, not the biggest number). The pain raises the stakes; the withheld answer forces the watch.
Mirror hooks work because specificity reads as "he's inside my head." So the lever is *specificity
about a real pain*, not clever wordplay. Vague = weak, always.

**Saveable vs shareable (and the tension Fraser raised).**
Fraser's instinct was right: educational content is *saveable* (I'll come back to this) but not
automatically *shareable* (I'll send this to my team). They're different jobs.
- Saveable = self-contained utility (a checklist, a framework, "the 5 things"). Saves = intent-to-use.
- Shareable = social currency (a hot take, a surprising teardown, an "oh my god look at this" like
  the AI-language reel). Shares = "this makes me look smart / this proves my point to my boss."
The move isn't "make everything both." It's know which job a given piece is doing and optimise for
*that* signal. Georgia's resolution — shareable = "would a marketer send it to their team?" — is the
useful lens. AI/novelty content over-indexes on shares; frameworks over-index on saves. Use both on purpose.

**The freebie + comment-keyword CTA.**
Double mechanism: (1) builds an *owned* audience (email) so you're not renting reach from Instagram;
(2) the comment-for-X mechanic manufactures the engagement signal that makes the algorithm push the
reel. So a CTA isn't decoration — it's fuel. But it only works if the freebie genuinely matches the
content's promise; a mismatched freebie kills trust and conversion. Match freebie → pillar → hook.

**Talking-head on the main feed vs B-roll trial reels.**
Two different jobs again. Trial reels = *acquire* cold followers cheaply (B-roll + hook). Main feed
= *convert* the follower into belief (you, on camera, being the expert — verbal + visual + text hook).
Fraser's data: long-form talking flops as trial reels. That's not a contradiction — it's the two jobs.
Don't put a convert-asset in an acquire-slot.

## Deeper WHYs Georgia developed on the call (the reasoning under the rules)

These came through richer in conversation than in her email summary. They're principles, not tactics.

**The stranger test — clarity is the actual product of a profile.**
Georgia kept returning to *"as a new visitor, I'm not immediately sure what I'll get consistently
from you."* That's the real diagnosis — not "the feed is messy" but *the positioning is unreadable in
3 seconds.* This reframes a pile of small tactics (bio line 1 = who/what, cover frames with the text
hook, highlights per pillar, consistent filming location) as one job: **a stranger lands and instantly
knows what they'll get and why you're worth following.** The bio, the grid, the highlights, the cover
photos aren't separate checklist items — they're all the same landing-page moment. Judge any profile
change by: *does a cold visitor get it faster now?*

**Consistency is the accelerator — not the volume itself, the compounding.**
Her read on Fraser's history: the old reels were *good*, they just weren't consistent, so nothing
compounded. *"You were throwing stuff at the wall."* The lever isn't talent (he has it) — it's showing
up in the same lane repeatedly so the audience, the algorithm, and the positioning all reinforce. This
is why "more volume" is her main prescription for someone already skilled. Reps in one direction > occasional brilliance.

**Follow the trail — don't reinvent, double down.**
*"Follow the trail of what's already worked rather than starting from scratch."* The reasoning:
proven winners already survived the market's judgment, so replicating their *style and topic* is lower
risk than inventing cold. She'd rather replicate than repost. For Fraser specifically this is huge —
he has a giant proven back-catalogue, so the default input to any piece is *a thing that already worked*,
adapted to IG. Inventing from zero is the rare fallback (same principle the anti-slop skill is built on:
references beat cold generation).

**Why the hook is ~everything.**
*"As long as you've grasped their attention in the first three to five seconds, you're already winning.
That's all that really matters."* The whole system is gated on the hook because nothing downstream —
the insight, the CTA, the proof — gets seen if the first 3 seconds fail. That's *why* trial reels exist
(a rig to test hooks cheaply), *why* all three hook layers matter (text catches mute-scrollers, verbal +
visual catch the rest), and *why* specificity is the lever. Everything else is secondary to the stop.

**Mirror hooks — the mechanism is recognition, and specificity is the dial.**
*"The more specific you can be, the more triggering it is — they're like 'oh my god, how do you know
that?'"* You're mirroring the viewer's exact lived experience back at them. Generic pain = ignored;
a precisely-named pain = *he's in my head* = the stop. So when brainstorming hooks, the work is getting
specific about a real pain the buyer actually has — not finding clever phrasing. (Her own method: start
from the pain, use Claude/ChatGPT to go *deeper* into the everyday version of it.)

**Georgia's own anti-slop point — and why it matters here.**
Unprompted, she said: *"There's a difference between getting something written up on ChatGPT and doing
it yourself. When people can't sense any personality or real-life struggle or insight behind the writing,
it feels generic — integrate as much of you as you can."* This is the same doctrine as the anti-slop kit,
from the strategist's mouth. It's why the whole voice apparatus exists: **generic writing doesn't just
read badly, it breaks the authority the content is trying to build.** Personality isn't garnish; it's proof
you actually know the thing.

**The order of belief — expertise first, personal second.**
*"People need a reason to follow you. Once they see you're an expert, they'll naturally get interested in
your personal life."* This is why Founder content is only 15% and framed as *supporting* authority. The
sequence matters: lead with proof of expertise, and the personal content then deepens a relationship that
already has a reason to exist. Personal-first would be building a house with no foundation.

**Content bank — the real enemy is the blank-screen decision.**
*"The biggest mistake creators make is deciding what to post every time they open Instagram — we go into
procrastination mode."* The bank isn't admin; it's removing the daily decision that kills consistency.
Brain-dump without filtering (every question asked, lesson, mistake, myth, advice repeated) because
*filtering at capture-time is where good raw material dies.*

**One idea → many formats — the multiplier that makes volume sustainable.**
*"One topic can become multiple pieces."* One insight = a talking-head reel + a storytelling carousel +
several trial-reel hooks + a Nice Ads subject line. This is *how* you hit the volume consistency demands
without inventing endlessly — you mine each proven idea fully across formats before moving on.

**Friction is the hidden killer (why captions/product names go on screen).**
*"Put it on screen so I don't have to go Google what I thought I heard you say."* Small point, general
principle: every bit of friction between the viewer and the value costs a save/share. Reducing
comprehension effort *is* optimising for the saveable/shareable signals. Applies everywhere — clarity
compounds.

## Fraser's reality overrides generic advice (where they clash)

Georgia gives the general model. Fraser has *data*. When they conflict, trust the data and reason about *why*:

- **AI content is a double-edged sword.** It reliably performs (top YouTube topics, the language reel)
  — but Fraser has seen it pull the *wrong* audience: quick-buck / AI-tourist types, not brand
  operators. So AI content is high-leverage *only when framed for operators* ("how a real agency uses
  this in the workflow"), not "AI hacks." Volume of views ≠ value if they're the wrong views. This is
  the master test beating a vanity metric.
- **TikTok skepticism.** Georgia says cross-post everywhere (marginal cost ≈ 0, fair). Fraser thinks
  TikTok's audience skews off-target. Both can be true: cross-post because it's free, but don't *design*
  for TikTok or read its signals as gospel for this buyer.
- **He's already good on camera.** Georgia's own note: his past reels weren't rambly, he's a strong
  talker. So the constraint isn't skill — it's *consistency and volume*. Don't over-coach delivery;
  optimise for reps and hooks.
- **He has a huge proven back-catalogue** (YouTube, Nice Ads, 10k+ ads made). The scarce input is never
  "ideas" — it's turning existing proven material into IG-native format. Default to *adapting a proven
  winner*, not inventing cold.

## The system (how the pieces reinforce, not a checklist)

Cold stranger → **trial reel** (cheap hook test) → follows → sees **main-feed talking-head** (belief) →
**comment-keyword** → **freebie** (owned email + engagement fuel) → nurtured → **course ($900)** →
some graduate to **agency ($10k+/mo)**. Nice Ads (12k subs) already feeds the top and drives IG traffic.

Each pillar has a job in this machine:
- **Why Ads Work (40%)** — manufactures authority at scale (the belief engine)
- **Inside the Ad World (25%)** — makes the agency tangible + humanises (de-risks hiring)
- **Client Wins (20%)** — proof; the closer; teaches-while-selling
- **Founder (15%)** — trust/relationship so authority doesn't feel cold

Percentages are a *balance heuristic*, not a quota. If a Client Win post is the strongest thing you've
got this week, run it — don't hold it back to protect a 20% ratio.

## How to decide / when to deviate

1. **Run the master test first.** Does this deepen "Fraser understands ad creative"? If not, fix or bin it.
2. **Name the job.** Acquire (trial reel) or convert (feed)? Save-optimised or share-optimised? Optimise
   for that piece's actual signal, not all of them at once.
3. **Specific beats clever.** Real numbers, real brands, real pains. Fraser's own voice doc + the
   anti-slop check enforce this — but the reason is trust, and trust is the whole product.
4. **Prefer adapting a proven winner** over inventing. The back-catalogue is the moat.
5. **Deviate openly.** If Georgia's rule doesn't fit the goal or Fraser's data, say so and explain the
   reasoning — don't silently follow or silently ignore. Fraser wants a thinking partner, not a rule-executor.
