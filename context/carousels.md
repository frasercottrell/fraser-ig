# Carousels — The Complete Guide

Everything the repo knows about making and writing Instagram carousels, pulled together in one
place. Sources: Georgia Socials call 1 + the call-2 email (`sources/georgia-call-2-email.md`),
`instagram-playbook.md`, `hooks-swipe.md`, `idea-bank.md`, `pillars.md`, `tone-of-voice.md`,
`ai-writing-tells.md`, `business.md`, and our own trial-reel data in `results-log.md`.

**Status: zero carousels posted.** Every number in here comes from trial reels and talking heads.
The transfer logic is stated where it's used, but nothing below is proven *on carousels yet*. First
few are experiments, not executions.

---

## 1. What a carousel is actually for

Carousels are the **third format**, alongside main-feed talking heads (convert) and trial reels
(acquire). Georgia's read: **they're under-used right now, which is exactly why they're worth
testing.**

The job: **the save.** A carousel is a self-contained, re-readable artefact. That makes it the most
naturally *saveable* format we have. Saves have been the bottleneck across the whole account
(reel 003 got 3, most got 0), and saves are what the operating model calls intent-to-use, the
signal that we attracted the right person rather than the biggest number.

Where it sits in the machine: cold stranger → trial reel → follow → **carousel / talking head
(belief + save)** → comment keyword → freebie → owned email → course → agency.

**Cadence:** 1–2 per week (call 1) rising to **1–3 per week, one per pillar** (call 2).

## 2. The slide-by-slide anatomy (the standard)

Straight from the call-2 email. This is the spec, not a suggestion.

| Slide | Job |
|---|---|
| **1** | Big pattern-interrupting hook. One job: stop the scroll. |
| **2** | Reinforce the hook / open a curiosity gap so they *have* to swipe to slide 3. |
| **3–7** | Tell the story, break down the insight, give real examples. Show the ad or the evidence. |
| **Final** | Strong CTA: "Comment [WORD] and I'll send you [relevant resource]." |

**Key objective, in Georgia's words:** make slides 1 and 2 so strong that someone who sees them
*feels like they need to swipe to find out the answer* — then use the rest to deliver enough value
that the final comment-for-freebie feels natural rather than tacked on.

**The two-hook structure is the thing that makes carousels different.** A reel has one hook. A
carousel has two: slide 1 buys the stop, slide 2 buys the swipe. Slide 2 is where carousels are
won and lost. If slides 1 and 2 aren't strong enough that someone needs the answer, **nothing after
them matters** — the best slide 5 in the world is never seen.

Max length: **8–10 slides.** 5 slides is the call-1 default and a fine floor.

## 3. Slide 1 — the stop

Same hook doctrine as everything else. **Identity → Pain → Curiosity.**

What our own data says about which hooks stop (n=17 trial reels, skip rate, lower is better):

| Hook style | Skip | What it converted |
|---|---|---|
| **Identity mirror** ("your agency can't tell you why your best ad worked…") | **35.1%** ✅ | big reach, 0 saves |
| Personal story ("my biggest mistake…") | 40.3% | low. Underperforms cold — save it for post-follow |
| Authority framework ("I studied thousands…") | 48.6% | **1 follow, 3 saves, a keyword lead** |
| Abstract aphorism ("the best copywriter is your customer…") | **62.3%** ❌ | nothing |

**The rule:** the more the hook names the **viewer** and a **sharp, felt tension**, the better it
stops. Clever loses to specific, every time. Abstract insight that requires you to already care is
the worst-performing shape we've tested.

**The fusion move** (the open experiment, and carousels are the ideal place to run it): an
identity/"you" hook on slide 1 (max stop) in front of saveable framework content on slides 3–7 (max
conversion). Reel 001 had the hook without the payoff; reel 003 had the payoff without the hook.
A carousel can carry both.

### Build slide 1 from a hook family, not from scratch

Georgia's headline unlock: **stop inventing a brand-new hook every time.** Build repeatable
families and test angles inside them, so a win teaches you something you can repeat.

The 3 families already proven on Fraser's account:

1. **Contrarian expertise** — "The first thing I check in a broken ad account isn't the targeting."
2. **Competitive gap / FOMO** — "Your competitor is testing 10x more ads than you. They're using AI."
3. **Pattern recognition / authority** — "The one thing every high-converting ad has in common."

The 5 fill-in templates to reuse forever:
- "Most brands think X. Actually, Y."
- "The first thing I check/do isn't X…"
- "Your competitor is doing X. Here's why."
- "Every winning X I've seen does Y."
- "I analysed X and noticed Y."

**Other rules that carry over:**
- Use the word **"ads"** in nearly every hook. It trains the algorithm on what Fraser talks about.
- **Be specific, and lead with the result in the first beat.** "My client tripled sales when we
  started using X" beats a general claim.
- **Name the brand and make a hard claim.** Reel 017 ran "GRÜNS is doing what 90% of wellness brands
  get wrong" over the drafted vague-curiosity line, and it became the best-performing talking head
  (first shares, first reposts, biggest reach). Specific + contrarian beats vague-curiosity.
- **Cut the tails.** "…here's why" and "…." defer instead of hitting. Reel 004 died with one.
- Never open with "I" (tone doc). Start mid-claim, mid-observation, or mid-scene.
- Full swipe file by category, including the agency call-out, founder and ecommerce hooks that are
  Fraser's strongest identity angles: `hooks-swipe.md`.

### Slide 1 is a cover photo, so the visual is the other half of the stop

Our single most-confirmed finding across 17 reels: **the first frame decides the stop.** Wide,
unclear, subject-in-the-background = 64% skip. Tight, legible, slightly *unusual* focal point =
28–33% skip. Confirmed three separate times.

Slide 1 of a carousel is the grid thumbnail and the feed's first frame. Same rule applies: a tight,
clear, curious image with the text legible over it. **Winning imagery = camera / behind-the-scenes
footage that implies real ad production** (the camera rig, the "fake shower" shoot). Georgia:
*"camera stuff does really well… anything behind the scenes implies big ads."*

## 4. Slide 2 — the swipe (the make-or-break)

The slide nobody writes carefully and the one that decides the post.

Slide 1 stopped them. Slide 2's only job is to make the *cost of not swiping* feel higher than the
swipe. It does that by **widening the gap it just opened, not by starting to close it.**

The failure mode is obvious once you name it: slide 2 begins answering. It gives the first point of
the framework, or defines terms, or restates slide 1 in longer words. Every one of those *reduces*
the tension the hook created, and there's now no reason to keep going.

What slide 2 can do instead:
- **Raise the stakes.** Put a number, a cost, or a consequence on the thing slide 1 named. "Most
  brands find out three months and £40k too late."
- **Deny the obvious answer.** Name what they think the answer is and take it off the table. This is
  the contrarian-expertise family working at slide level: *it isn't the targeting. It isn't the
  budget. It's not even the offer.*
- **Add specificity that proves you actually know.** The mirror-hook mechanism: *"how do you know
  that?"* Specificity about a real pain is the dial, not clever phrasing.
- **Promise the shape of the payoff without the payoff.** "There are five of them. The fifth one is
  the one nobody believes."
- **Show the evidence, unexplained.** Put the ad, the dashboard, the number on screen and let it beg
  the question.

Test for slide 2: cover slides 3 onward. Does someone who has read only slides 1 and 2 *need* the
answer? If the honest answer is "it'd be mildly interesting", rewrite it.

## 5. Slides 3–7 — the payload

Tell the story, break down the insight, give real examples.

**Make it a saveable framework.** This is the most reliable finding in the results log: **saveable
framework content converts to saves and follows; hot takes get reach and zero saves.** A numbered
breakdown (the 3 jobs of a hook, the 5 things every winner did, 3 signs of fatigue) is what people
save. Abstract insight gets neither reach nor saves.

**Show the ad or the evidence on screen.** Georgia, on ad breakdowns: showing real examples builds
authority and the algorithm rewards it. In carousel terms, slides 3–7 should carry actual ad frames,
screenshots, numbers, the dashboard. Don't describe the ad. Put it on the slide.

**Notes-app style, bite-size, written like a human.** Write it like you're talking to a friend.
Aggressive line breaks. One idea per slide. Short paragraphs and fragments, not a paragraph of body
copy squeezed onto an image.

**Friction is the hidden killer.** Georgia: *"put it on screen so I don't have to go Google what I
thought I heard you say."* Product names, tool names, metrics, brand names go on the slide. Every
bit of comprehension effort costs a save.

**Specificity rule (tone doc).** Real numbers beat vague claims. "This one change dropped CPA by
24%" over "we significantly improved performance." No number to hand? Use a concrete detail — name
the platform, the format, the niche, the funnel stage.

**Structures Fraser likes**, all of which map cleanly onto slides 3–7:
- Hook → Insight → Real example → Takeaway
- Myth → Truth → What to do instead
- "We used to do X. Now we do Y. Here's why."
- Step-by-step walkthrough, zero fluff

## 6. The final slide — the CTA

Format: **"Comment [WORD] and I'll send you [relevant resource]."**

**The correction from call 2, and it changes what we've been doing: the freebie must be relevant to
the content.** Don't put HOOKS on a carousel that isn't about hooks. Rotate 2–3 freebies and tag the
matching one.

**What's actually live right now — only one:**
- ✅ **HOOKS** → "Comment HOOKS and I'll send you 34 proven hook templates to start from."

**Not built. Do not promise these:**
- ⛔ SWIPE (annotated ad swipe file) · ⛔ CONVERT ("why your ads don't convert" PDF) · ⛔ WHY (the 5
  questions that reveal whether your agency knows what's working)

**Rule until more exist:** default to HOOKS and favour carousel topics where HOOKS is a genuine
match. When the topic doesn't fit, use a **soft CTA** — a comment-to-engage question or a plain
follow. **A CTA that promises an undelivered freebie is worse than no CTA.**

Why the CTA is not decoration: it's a double mechanism. It builds the owned email list *and* it
manufactures the comment engagement that makes the algorithm push the post. But a mismatched freebie
kills trust and conversion, which is why relevance beats volume here.

Also on the final slide: the follow CTA. "Follow @frasercottrell for more from 10,000+ ads made."

## 7. Design and craft

- **Image + basic readable text.** Lower the photo's exposure so the text reads cleanly. Legibility
  over aesthetics, always.
- **Notes-app aesthetic** is the reference. Bite-size, human, unpolished.
- **Trending audio** — look for the ↑ arrow. Yes, on carousels too.
- **Max 8–10 slides.** Five is a fine floor.
- **Open test: same photo throughout vs a different photo per slide.** Nobody knows yet. Run it as a
  named variable.
- Consistent, recognisable location in the imagery (the profile-level "stranger test" — a cold
  visitor should recognise Fraser's setup at a glance).

## 8. The caption

Carousels get the **full caption standard**, same bar as reels. This is where the save is finally
earned, and the old short captions were the thing Georgia pushed back on.

- **Much longer.** Pack it with genuinely useful, specific info — the kind of thing a marketer
  screenshots and comes back to *right before they run their ads*. If it's not worth saving, it's
  not done.
- **A different caption for every hook.** No recycling.
- **Listicles, aim for 5 points, not 3.** Each point is 2–3 sentences of real tactical advice: a
  specific example, a concrete number, a do-this-not-that. Not one-liners.
- **Genuinely helpful, not surface.** Name the niche, the phrase, the metric, the exact fix. Teach
  something a pro would still find useful.
- **Keep the voice inside the longer structure.** Long doesn't mean padded. Every line earns its
  place.
- **First line is the driver.** Only it shows before "…more", so it *is* the caption CTA. It must
  promise the payoff ("Save this before you write your next ad 👇"), never warm up.
- **Keyword-stuff with real industry terms** for reach.
- **Two CTAs at the end:** follow + comment keyword.

Worked examples of the standard live in `posts/batch-12-long-captions.md` and
`posts/batch-13-fused-identity.md`.

**The test:** would someone about to launch an ad save this to reference? If not, add more real
substance.

## 9. Voice and the anti-slop check

Full doctrine in `tone-of-voice.md` and `ai-writing-tells.md`. The bits that bite hardest on slide
copy, where every word is exposed:

- **No em-dashes.** The loudest AI tell. Recast as a full stop, a comma, or split the sentence. The
  linter flags every single one.
- **No negative parallelism or balanced triads.** "It's not just X, it's Y." "Style, comfort, and
  durability." Models default to these; slide copy is where they're most visible.
- **No rhetorical self-questions** you immediately answer. "Sound familiar?" "The result?" A real
  question hook is fine; the fake one is a tell.
- **No signposted transitions.** "But here's the thing." "That's where X comes in."
- **No participle tails.** "…, ensuring your ads convert."
- **No inflated significance, no puffery, no vague attribution.** "Experts agree" is both a tell and
  a compliance risk.
- **No manufactured intimacy.** "Can I be real for a second?" Just be it.
- Banned outright: "unlock", "leverage", "supercharge", "elevate", "game-changer", "I'm excited to
  share", hashtag spam.

Georgia said the same thing unprompted, from the strategist's side: *"When people can't sense any
personality or real-life struggle or insight behind the writing, it feels generic."* Generic writing
doesn't just read badly, it breaks the authority the content exists to build.

**Workflow:** draft → `python3 scripts/voice-lint.py path/to/draft.md` → fix real flags by rewriting
in Fraser's register (not thesaurus-swapping) → sanity-check against the tells doc.

## 10. Testing discipline

Georgia: *"Focus on testing, not just posting."* For every carousel, write down **the one variable
you're testing** and log it next to the numbers in `results-log.md`:

- **Hook** — same topic and format, different hook family
- **Angle** — same claim, different way in
- **Format** — **trial reel vs main reel vs carousel on the *same* hook.** This is the big one for
  carousels: it's the only clean way to learn what the format itself is worth
- **Topic** — which pillar cold viewers actually bite on

One variable at a time. Change the hook *and* the imagery *and* the topic and a win tells you
nothing.

**Which metrics to judge on.** Match the signal to the job (operating model). A carousel is a
save-optimised, belief-building asset on the main feed, so:
- **Saves** are the primary signal. Self-contained utility, intent-to-use.
- **Shares** for teardowns and contrarian pieces. Reel 017 showed teardowns get forwarded where hot
  takes don't, because a teardown is *useful to send to your team*.
- **Comments** from the keyword CTA.
- Skip rate is a trial-reel metric. It does not apply here.

Trial reels and main-feed content are **separate scoreboards.** Don't compare a carousel's numbers
to a trial reel's.

## 11. The 3 carousels to build first

Georgia's brief, verbatim intent:

**1. "Most brands are wasting money because XYZ."**
Reveal the mistake → show how brands *should* be creating and testing ads → give examples of what to
do instead. (Note: reel 015 already ran "Most brands are wasting money because of this" as a trial
reel, so there's a same-hook format comparison available for free.)

**2. "I've tested 10,000+ ads. This is what actually works."**
Use the number as credibility, then break down the patterns repeated across winning ads. Pairs with
the talking head that already ran on this ("ugly ads win", reel 016) and the batch-12 caption that
already has the 5-point framework written.

**3. Bonus / wildcard.**
Any story inside the pillars. A pure experiment to read what cold viewers bite on. Tag the test
variable in the results log.

Both #1 and #2 fit HOOKS as a genuine CTA match, which matters while it's the only live freebie.

## 12. Where carousel topics come from

**Don't invent. Adapt a proven winner.** Georgia: *"Follow the trail of what's already worked rather
than starting from scratch."* Fraser's back-catalogue is the moat, and the scarce input is never
ideas — it's converting proven material into IG-native format.

**The multiplier:** one topic = a talking-head reel + a carousel + several trial-reel hooks + a Nice
Ads newsletter title. Mine each idea fully across formats before moving on. That's how the volume
gets sustainable.

Highest-confidence sources to pull from:
- **`results-log.md`** — any reel that already won. Reel 009 (audit, 650 reach), reel 010 (AI
  competitor, best skip + a follow), reel 014, reel 017 (Grüns teardown, first shares).
- **`proven-content.md`** — the YouTube back-catalogue: "I tested 500 static ads", the Andromeda
  guide, "copy this Meta ad strategy", "how to make AI image ads", the AI-language reel.
- **`idea-bank.md`** — the full topic list by pillar, plus the call-2 ideas: 3 types of statistic-led
  ads that scale, how AI is changing creative testing, what founders get wrong, how to turn a
  founder's story into an ad, fake podcast ads, yapper ads.
- **`pillars.md`** — one carousel per pillar per week is the target shape. Pillar 1 (Why Ads Work,
  40%) is the authority engine and the natural home for framework carousels.

**Two live cautions.** AI content performs but pulls the wrong audience if framed as hacks — frame
it for operators. And do **not** use Steven Bartlett / Huel in any breakdown (legal history).

## 13. Build checklist

Before it goes out:

- [ ] Slide 1 names the **viewer** and a **sharp, felt pain**. Not an aphorism. No "I" opener, no
      trailing "…here's why".
- [ ] Slide 1 uses the word "ads" and comes from one of the 3 hook families.
- [ ] Slide 1's image is a tight, legible, slightly unusual frame. BTS / camera / production.
- [ ] **Slide 2 widens the gap.** Cover slides 3+ and check someone *needs* the answer.
- [ ] Slides 3–7 are a numbered, saveable framework, one idea per slide.
- [ ] Real evidence on screen: the ad, the number, the dashboard. Not described, shown.
- [ ] Specific numbers and named brands throughout. No vague claims.
- [ ] Final slide CTA matches a freebie that **actually exists** (HOOKS, or soft CTA).
- [ ] Follow CTA present.
- [ ] Caption is long, 5-point listicle, genuinely tactical, different from every other caption.
- [ ] Caption first line promises the payoff.
- [ ] Photo exposure lowered, text legible on every slide.
- [ ] Trending audio (↑).
- [ ] ≤ 10 slides.
- [ ] `voice-lint.py` run and real flags rewritten. Zero em-dashes.
- [ ] **The one variable being tested is written down** and logged.
- [ ] Master test: does this deepen "Fraser understands ad creative"?
- [ ] Save test: would someone about to launch an ad save this?

## 14. What we don't know yet

Honest gaps, so nobody mistakes inference for evidence:

- **No carousel has been posted.** Everything here is transferred from trial reels and two talking
  heads. Treat the first three as experiments.
- **Whether the first-frame finding transfers.** It's a scroll-stop mechanism in a video feed. It
  should hold on a carousel cover, but it's untested.
- **Same photo throughout vs different photos per slide.** Named as a test by Georgia, never run.
- **Optimal length.** 5 vs 8 vs 10 slides. No data.
- **Whether carousels beat reels on the same hook.** The single most valuable test available, and
  the reason to run at least one carousel on a hook that already has trial-reel numbers.
- **Whether trending audio does anything on a carousel.** Assumed from the reel playbook.
